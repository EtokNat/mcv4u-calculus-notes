Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Follow the five-step optimization algorithm.
  • Set up area, volume, and distance models with a labelled diagram and a correct domain.
  • Interpret endpoints that give impossible results.

Minds On

We frequently need to do the best we can — minimize cost, maximize area, minimize distance. This is optimization: the best outcome subject to a set of restrictions.

Restrictions give the function a restricted domain — the maximum or minimum can occur in the interior or at the endpoints.

Part 1 — The algorithm

Algorithm for Solving Optimization Problems

  1. Identify what varies. Write the quantity to optimize as a function of one variable.
  2. Whenever possible, draw a labelled diagram.
  3. Determine the domain from the constraints.
  4. Apply the extreme-value algorithm on the domain.
  5. Answer the original question (not just the critical \(x\)).

Part 2 — Worked example: the fence

Example — 800 m of fencing, 3 sides

A farmer has 800 m of fencing and wants to enclose a rectangular field. One side is along a country road (already fenced). Maximize the area.

Step 1 — one variable. Let the width perpendicular to the road be \(x\) m. Then the length along the road is \(800 - 2x\) m.

Step 2 — model. \(A(x) = x(800 - 2x) = 800x - 2x^{2}.\)

Step 3 — domain. \(0 < x < 400.\)

Example — apply the extreme-value algorithm

Step 4a. \(A'(x) = 800 - 4x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 200.\)

Step 4b — evaluate.

\(A(0) = 0,\;\; A(200) = 200(400) = 80\,000,\;\; A(400) = 0.\)

The endpoints give zero-area "rectangles" — physically impossible.

Step 5 — answer. Maximum area \(= 80\,000\) m², with field 200 m × 400 m.

Classwork

Three questions from Bronston's Check Your Understanding (Lesson 3.4).

~10 min total. Each practises a different constraint shape.

C1 — Wire bent into a rectangle

A 100 cm wire is bent into a rectangle. What dimensions give the maximum area?

Step 1. Let one side \(= x\). Perimeter \(= 100 \Rightarrow\) other side \(= 50 - x.\)

Step 2. \(A(x) = x(50 - x).\) Domain \(0 < x < 50.\)

Step 3. \(A'(x) = 50 - 2x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 25.\)

25 cm × 25 cm square (area \(= 625\) cm²).

C2 — Rectangle of area 64 m², minimum perimeter

A rectangle has area 64 m². What dimensions give the smallest perimeter?

Step 1. Let one side \(= x\). Other side \(= 64/x.\)

Step 2. \(P(x) = 2x + \dfrac{128}{x}.\) Domain \(x > 0.\)

Step 3. \(P'(x) = 2 - \dfrac{128}{x^{2}} = 0 \Rightarrow x^{2} = 64 \Rightarrow x = 8.\)

8 m × 8 m square (perimeter \(= 32\) m).

C3 — 600 m of fence beside a river (3 sides)

What dimensions maximize the enclosed area?

Step 1. Width (perpendicular to river) \(= x\); length (parallel) \(= 600 - 2x.\)

Step 2. \(A(x) = x(600 - 2x).\) Domain \(0 < x < 300.\)

Step 3. \(A'(x) = 600 - 4x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 150.\)

150 m × 300 m  (max area \(= 45\,000\) m²).

Homework

Bronston Formative Check B — geometric optimization pool.

Cover each answer before attempting. Final answers only — show your working in your book.

H1 — Bacteria in a pool

A pool is treated for bacteria. \(t\) days after treatment, the count is \(N(t) = 30t^{2} - 240t + 500.\)

Determine the lowest number of bacteria during the first week.

Lowest number \(= 20\) per cm³ (at \(t = 4\) days).

H2 — Largest rectangle inscribed in a right triangle

Legs along the \(x\)- and \(y\)-axes; sides of the rectangle lie along the legs.

(a) Legs \(5\) cm and \(12\) cm. Largest area \(=\) ?    (b) Legs \(8\) cm and \(15\) cm. Largest area \(=\) ?

(c) Hypothesize a conclusion for any right triangle.

(a) \(15\) cm²    (b) \(30\) cm²
(c) The largest rectangle inscribed in a right triangle has area equal to half the triangle's area.

H3 — Square-based rectangular box, \(V = 1000\) cm³

Minimize the surface area. (All dimensions \(\ge 2\) cm; no waste.)

The box is a cube with side \(10\) cm.
Minimum surface area \(= 600\) cm².

H4 — Golf-net enclosure, \(V = 144\) m³

Net on the sides, top, and far end only (open at one end). Square cross-section.

Minimize the netting.

Square end side \(= 6\) m, length \(= 4\) m.

H5 — Minimum distance from \((-3, 3)\) to \(y = (x - 3)^{2}\)

Find the minimum distance from the point to the curve.

Minimum distance \(= \sqrt{17}\).

Wrap-up — Lesson 3.4

Key ideas

  • Optimization = extreme-value algorithm applied to a model you build.
  • One variable → labelled diagram → domain → extreme-value algorithm → answer the original question.

Need to know

  • Endpoints can hold the optimum — but confirm they are physically possible.
