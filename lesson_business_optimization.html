Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Build a revenue function \(R = (\text{price})(\text{quantity})\) from market research.
  • Build a cost function from per-unit material prices + a volume constraint.
  • Use marginal revenue \(R'(x)\) and marginal cost \(C'(x)\) to analyze profit.
  • Respect practical domain constraints (capacity, integer steps, feasibility).

Minds On

In business, we want to maximize profit (revenue minus cost). Antagonizing customers with high prices hurts future revenue — so practical constraints matter too.

Revenue \(= (\text{price per unit}) \times (\text{units sold})\).

Marginal cost \(= C'(x)\); marginal revenue \(= R'(x)\); profit peaks where \(R'(x) = C'(x).\)

Part 1 — Maximizing revenue

Example — Commuter train fare

A train carries 2000 passengers daily at $7.00 per person.

  • Each \($0.10\) increase loses 40 passengers.
  • Each \($0.10\) decrease gains 40 passengers.
  • Capacity \(= 2600\); break-even minimum \(= 1600\) passengers.

What fare maximizes daily revenue?

Example — build the revenue function

Step 1. Let \(x\) \(= \) number of \($0.10\) increases (\(x\) can be negative).

Step 2. fare \(= 7 + 0.10x\); passengers \(= 2000 - 40x\).

Step 3 — domain.

\(2000 - 40x \ge 1600 \Rightarrow x \le 10\).

\(2000 - 40x \le 2600 \Rightarrow x \ge -15\). So \(-15 \le x \le 10\).

Step 4. \(R(x) = (7 + 0.10x)(2000 - 40x) = -4x^{2} - 80x + 14\,000.\)

Example — apply the extreme-value algorithm

\(R'(x) = -8x - 80 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -10\) (inside the domain).

\(R(-15) = 14\,300;\quad R(-10) = 14\,400;\quad R(10) = 12\,800.\)

Maximum revenue \(= \$14\,400\) at \(x = -10\): a \(\$1.00\) decrease.
New fare: \$6.00; passengers: 2400.

Counter-intuitive but mathematical: lowering the fare brought in enough extra passengers to beat the higher fare.

Classwork — Revenue & Profit

Two questions from Bronston's Check Your Understanding (Lesson 3.5).

~8 min total.

C1 — CD player price

CD players sell at $90 (50 units per 2 weeks). Each $1 price cut sells 5 more units. What price maximizes revenue?

Step 1. Let \(x\) \(= \) number of $1 decreases. Price \(= 90 - x\); units \(= 50 + 5x.\)

Step 2. \(R(x) = (90 - x)(50 + 5x) = -5x^{2} + 400x + 4500.\)

Step 3. \(R'(x) = -10x + 400 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 40.\)

Price \(= \$50\)   (units \(= 250\); revenue \(= \$12\,500\)).

C2 — MP3 player profit

Store cost \($70\); current price \($100\); 120 sold/month. Each \($2\) increase loses 1 sale. Maximize profit.

Step 1. Let \(x\) \(= \) number of $2 increases. Units \(= 120 - x\); profit per unit \(= 30 + 2x.\)

Step 2. \(P(x) = (120 - x)(30 + 2x) = -2x^{2} + 210x + 3600.\)

Step 3. \(P'(x) = -4x + 210 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 52.5.\)

Price \(= 100 + 2(52.5) = \$205.\)

Part 2 — Minimizing cost

Example — Cylindrical storage tank

Capacity \(1000\) m³; warehouse \(12\times 15\times 11\) m.

Base \(\$100\)/m², top \(\$50\)/m², wall \(\$80\)/m². Minimize cost.

Feasibility. \(r \le 6\) (half the width); max volume \(\pi(6)^{2}(11) \approx 1244 \ge 1000\). Fits.

Radius domain. With \(h = 11\), \(\pi r^{2}(11) \ge 1000 \Rightarrow r \ge 5.38\). So \(5.38 \le r \le 6.\)

Example — build the cost function

\(C = 100(\pi r^{2}) + 50(\pi r^{2}) + 80(2\pi r h) = 150\pi r^{2} + 160\pi r h.\)

Constraint: \(V = \pi r^{2} h = 1000 \Rightarrow h = \dfrac{1000}{\pi r^{2}}.\)

Substitute:

\(C(r) = 150\pi r^{2} + \dfrac{160\,000}{r},\;\; 5.38 \le r \le 6.\)

Example — solve on \([5.38, 6]\)

\(C'(r) = 300\pi r - \dfrac{160\,000}{r^{2}} = 0 \Rightarrow r^{3} = \dfrac{1600}{3\pi} \Rightarrow r \approx 5.54.\)

Evaluate:

\(C(5.38) \approx 43\,380;\quad C(5.54) \approx 43\,344;\quad C(6) \approx 43\,631.\)

Minimum cost \(\approx \$43\,344\) at \(r \approx 5.54\) m, \(h \approx 10.37\) m.

C3 — What does marginal cost mean?

If marginal cost at some production level \(x\) is \(\$0.50\)/L, what does that mean practically?

Producing one more litre at that production level is expected to cost about \$0.50. Marginal cost \(C'(x)\) is the instantaneous rate of change of total cost.

Homework

Bronston Formative Check C — revenue, cost, and cost-function pool.

Cover each answer before attempting. Final answers only — show your working in your book.

H1 — Apartment rent

A 50-unit building has rent $850/month; all occupied. Every $100 increase causes 10 vacancies.

What rent maximizes revenue?

Rent \(= \$850\)/month (the current rent — any increase loses more revenue than it gains).

H2 — Basketball arena

Arena holds 20 000; 14 000 attend at $75/ticket. Each $5 cut adds 800 fans.

Find the price that maximizes revenue.

Price \(= \$81.25\) (the optimum is actually a slight increase).

H3 — Unit cost of manufacturing

Cost is \(C(x) = 625 + 15x + 0.01x^{2}\) for \(1 \le x \le 500.\)

Unit cost \(U(x) = C(x)/x.\) How many items should be made to minimize \(U(x)\)?

\(x = 250\) items.

H4 — Minimize average cost

For \(C(x) = 0.004x^{2} + 40x + 16\,000\), find the production level that minimizes the average cost per unit \(\overline{C}(x) = C(x)/x.\)

\(x = 2000\) units.

H5 — Cough and trachea radius

During a cough the trachea narrows. Air velocity is modelled by \(v(r) = Ar^{2}(r_{0} - r),\) where \(0 \le r \le r_{0}.\)

Find the radius giving maximum velocity, and the maximum velocity.

Radius \(= \dfrac{2r_{0}}{3}\);   maximum velocity \(= \dfrac{4Ar_{0}^{3}}{27}.\)

Wrap-up — Lessons 3.5 + 3.6

Key ideas

  • \(R = (\text{price})(\text{quantity})\); \(P = R - C\); \(P' = 0\) when \(R' = C'.\)
  • Cost functions: use the volume (or area) constraint to reduce to one variable before differentiating.
