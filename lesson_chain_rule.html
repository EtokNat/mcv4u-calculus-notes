The Chain Rule

MCV4U · Calculus and Vectors

Lesson 2 · differentiating compositions

What you should already know

  • Power rule: \(\dfrac{d}{dx}x^{n} = nx^{n-1}\)
  • Product rule: \((fg)' = f'g + fg'\)
  • Quotient rule (and the Ministry's \(fg^{-1}\) rewrite)
  • Basic trig derivatives: \(\sin'x = \cos x\), \(\cos'x = -\sin x\)

Why we need a new rule

Consider \(y = (2x+1)^{5}\). How do we differentiate this?

Old way: expand the binomial — tedious.

Now consider \(y = \sqrt{x^{2}+5}\).

We cannot expand a square root over a sum.

We need a rule for compositions — "functions inside functions."

The Chain Rule

If \(y = f(g(x))\), with \(g\) differentiable at \(x\) and \(f\) differentiable at \(g(x)\), then

\[\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f'\bigl(g(x)\bigr)\cdot g'(x).\]

Leibniz form: \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = \dfrac{dy}{du}\cdot\dfrac{du}{dx}\)   where \(u = g(x)\).

Memory phrase: "derivative of the outside (keep the inside), times the derivative of the inside."

The most common special case

Generalized power rule. If \(u = g(x)\), then

\[\dfrac{d}{dx}\bigl[u^{n}\bigr] = n\,u^{n-1}\cdot u'.\]

Any time you see "(stuff)\(^n\)," bring the \(n\) down, drop the exponent by one, and multiply by the derivative of the stuff.

Example 1 — verify on \(y = (5x^{3})^{1/3}\)

First, by simplifying: \(y = 5^{1/3}\,x\), so \(y' = 5^{1/3}\).

Step 1 — chain rule. Outside: \(u^{1/3}\). Inside: \(u = 5x^{3}\).

Step 2. \(\dfrac{d}{du}u^{1/3} = \tfrac{1}{3}u^{-2/3}\), and \(u' = 15x^{2}\).

Step 3. \(y' = \tfrac{1}{3}(5x^{3})^{-2/3}\cdot 15x^{2} = 5(5x^{3})^{-2/3}\,x^{2}\).

Step 4. \((5x^{3})^{2/3} = 5^{2/3}x^{2}\), so \(y' = \dfrac{5x^{2}}{5^{2/3}x^{2}} = 5^{1/3}\).

Both methods give \(y' = 5^{1/3}\). ✓

Example 2 — \(y = (2x+1)^{5}\)

Step 1. Outside: \(u^{5}\). Inside: \(u = 2x+1\).

Step 2. \(\dfrac{d}{du}u^{5} = 5u^{4}\), and \(u' = 2\).

Step 3. \(y' = 5(2x+1)^{4}\cdot 2\).

\(y' = 10(2x+1)^{4}.\)

Example 3 — \(y = \sqrt{x^{2}+5}\)

Step 1. Rewrite: \(y = (x^{2}+5)^{1/2}\).

Step 2. Outside: \(u^{1/2}\). Inside: \(u = x^{2}+5\).

Step 3. \(\dfrac{d}{du}u^{1/2} = \tfrac{1}{2}u^{-1/2}\), and \(u' = 2x\).

Step 4. \(y' = \tfrac{1}{2}(x^{2}+5)^{-1/2}\cdot 2x\).

\(y' = \dfrac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2}+5}}.\)

Example 4 — \(y = \sin(3x^{2})\)

Step 1. Outside: \(\sin u\). Inside: \(u = 3x^{2}\).

Step 2. \(\dfrac{d}{du}\sin u = \cos u\), and \(u' = 6x\).

Step 3. \(y' = \cos(3x^{2})\cdot 6x\).

\(y' = 6x\cos(3x^{2}).\)

Example 5 — \(y = x^{2}(2x+1)^{3}\)

Product rule with a chain rule inside.

Step 1. Let \(u = x^{2}\), \(v = (2x+1)^{3}\).

Step 2. \(u' = 2x\). For \(v'\): chain rule gives \(v' = 3(2x+1)^{2}\cdot 2 = 6(2x+1)^{2}\).

Step 3. Product rule: \(y' = 2x(2x+1)^{3} + x^{2}\cdot 6(2x+1)^{2}\).

Step 4. Factor \(2x(2x+1)^{2}\): \(y' = 2x(2x+1)^{2}\bigl[(2x+1) + 3x\bigr]\).

\(y' = 2x(2x+1)^{2}(5x+1).\)

Quotients the Ministry way

Ontario's curriculum derives quotient derivatives via \(f\cdot g^{-1}\) — rewrite the quotient as a product with a negative exponent, then use product + chain.

Pattern: \(\dfrac{f}{g} = f\cdot g^{-1}\), so

\[\left(\dfrac{f}{g}\right)' = f'\cdot g^{-1} + f\cdot(-1)g^{-2}\cdot g' = \dfrac{f'g - fg'}{g^{2}}.\]

Same quotient-rule formula — derived from product + chain.

Example 6 — \(y = \dfrac{x}{x^{2}+1}\) via product + chain

Step 1. Rewrite: \(y = x(x^{2}+1)^{-1}\).

Step 2. Let \(u = x\), \(v = (x^{2}+1)^{-1}\). Then \(u' = 1\).

Step 3. Chain: \(v' = -1(x^{2}+1)^{-2}\cdot 2x = \dfrac{-2x}{(x^{2}+1)^{2}}\).

Step 4. Product: \(y' = (1)(x^{2}+1)^{-1} + x\cdot\dfrac{-2x}{(x^{2}+1)^{2}}\).

Step 5. Common denom: \(y' = \dfrac{(x^{2}+1) - 2x^{2}}{(x^{2}+1)^{2}}\).

\(y' = \dfrac{1-x^{2}}{(x^{2}+1)^{2}}.\)
