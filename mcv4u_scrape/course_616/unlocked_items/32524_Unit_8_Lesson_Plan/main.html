UNIT 8 LESSON PLAN

Applications of Vectors

Course: MCV4U, Grade 12 Calculus and Vectors  |  Unit: 8 — Applications of Vectors (Nelson Ch. 7, applied)  |  Time: 10–12 periods (~75 min each)

Curriculum expectations (Strand C — Geometry and Algebra of Vectors, applied)

  • C2.1 — Solve force problems: resultant and equilibrant of concurrent forces; static equilibrium via force triangles and component balance (7.1).
  • C2.2 — Solve velocity problems: ground velocity = vehicle velocity + wind/current; river crossings via similar triangles; heading corrections (7.2).
  • C2.3 — Apply W = F·s to work problems: angled forces, multiple forces, negative work (7.7; Unit 7, Lesson 7.6).
  • C2.4 — Apply torque magnitude = |r||f|sinθ to torque problems: tools, doors, rotational balance (7.7; Unit 7, Lesson 7.6).
  • C2.5 — Apply projections in context: ramp components, useful/lifting pull components, headwind/tailwind (7.5; Unit 7, Lesson 7.3).
  • C2.6 — Apply vectors to geometry: distances from displacement magnitude; areas via |a × b| (and half for triangles); volumes via the scalar triple product (7.7; Unit 7, Lesson 7.5).

Learning Goals

Students will solve applied statics problems (resultant, equilibrant, equilibrium triangles); solve navigation problems (ground velocity, river crossings, heading corrections); compute work in multi-force and friction scenarios; compute torque and solve lever/seesaw balance problems; resolve vectors into parallel and perpendicular components in applied settings; and compute distances, areas, and volumes from coordinates.

Success criteria

  • I can draw a position diagram and a vector diagram for any force or velocity word problem before computing.
  • I can state the equilibrium condition (vector sum = 0) and the triangle-inequality feasibility test for three forces.
  • I can always check "wind from X" means the vector points the opposite way, and distinguish heading from course.
  • I can identify the angle θ in W = |F||s|cosθ as the angle between the force and the displacement, and give friction's work a negative sign.
  • I can explain why pushing at 90° and far from the pivot maximizes torque, and balance two torques on a lever.
  • I can split any vector into components along a chosen direction and interpret the sign of a projection.

Assessment plan

  • For learning: Entry Card (word-problem experience, diagram confidence, goals), Formative Checks A/B/C with answer-specific feedback.
  • As learning: Exit Card (self-assessment against entry goals), Unit 8 Discussion (peer argument: which vector concept explains a real device?).
  • Of learning: Summative Assignment (16 Q, 100 pts, hybrid auto/teacher-marked), Summative Test (26 Q, 100 pts, 120 min, hybrid).

Lesson sequence

  1. Entry Card → Lesson 8.1 (forces in equilibrium: resultant, equilibrant, force triangles, ramps).
  2. Lesson 8.2 (velocity and navigation: ground velocity, river crossings, heading corrections) → Formative Check A (7.1 + 7.2).
  3. Lesson 8.3 (work — applied problems: ramps, mowers, multiple pushers, friction) → Formative Check B (7.3 + 7.4).
  4. Lesson 8.4 (torque — applied problems: wrenches, doors, seesaws).
  5. Lesson 8.5 (projections in applications: ramp components, useful pull, headwind/tailwind).
  6. Lesson 8.6 (geometric applications: distances, areas, volumes) → Formative Check C (7.5 + 7.6).
  7. Summative Assignment → Summative Test → Discussion → Exit Card.

Materials

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 Ch. 7 (applied sections); Desmos (embedded in each lesson); curated Khan Academy video per lesson (all IDs verified public at build time); 14 original matplotlib diagrams in figures/ch8/.

Differentiation

Formative checks give instant targeted feedback for self-pacing. Each lesson cross-references the Unit 7 lesson where the underlying computation was taught, so students can review theory without re-learning it. Extension: the scalar triple product for volumes (Lesson 8.6) and the friction-on-ramp analysis (Formative C / Summative E6) go beyond the textbook chapter; the discussion task (design your own applied vector argument with a calculation) suits strong students. Support: every worked example starts from a labelled diagram; the Desmos explorations make the geometry visible before the algebra; and the "Minds On" paragraph in each lesson names the real-world situation first.
