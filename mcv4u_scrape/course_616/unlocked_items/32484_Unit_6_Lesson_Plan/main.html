UNIT 6 LESSON PLAN

Vectors

Course: MCV4U, Grade 12 Calculus and Vectors  |  Unit: 6 — Vectors (Nelson Ch. 6)  |  Time: 12–14 periods (~75 min each)

Curriculum expectations (Strand C — Geometry and Algebra of Vectors)

  • C1.1 — Recognize a vector as a quantity with both magnitude and direction; represent vectors as directed line segments (6.1).
  • C1.2 — Perform geometric vector operations: addition and subtraction via the triangle and parallelogram laws; scalar multiplication (6.2, 6.3).
  • C1.3 — Determine properties of vector operations: commutative, associative, and distributive laws (6.4).
  • C2.1 — Determine the Cartesian (component) representation of a vector in R² and R³, including position vectors (6.5).
  • C2.2 — Perform operations on algebraic vectors in R² and R³: componentwise addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication; convert between component and i, j, k forms (6.6, 6.7).
  • C2.3 — Express vectors as linear combinations; determine spanning sets and test coplanarity (6.8).

🎯 Learning Goals

Students will distinguish scalars from vectors and identify equal/opposite vectors; add and subtract geometric vectors with the triangle and parallelogram laws and compute resultant magnitudes with the cosine law; describe scalar-multiplication effects, test collinearity, and construct unit vectors; locate points and position vectors in R² and R³; perform componentwise operations and convert between component and i, j, k forms; and write linear combinations, test spanning sets, and check coplanarity.

Success criteria

  • I can state the two things that define a vector and explain why position is not one of them.
  • I can draw a + b both ways (parallelogram and triangle) and read a − b off a tail-to-tail diagram.
  • I can predict the direction and magnitude of ka for any real k, including k = 0.
  • I can find AB as head − tail and its magnitude in R² and R³ without a diagram.
  • I can convert between (a, b[, c]) and ai + bj [+ ck] and never scale only one component.
  • I can solve for the scalars in a linear combination and explain why a collinear set cannot span.

Assessment plan

  • For learning: Entry Card (feelings, goals, trig/coordinate confidence), Formative Checks A/B/C with answer-specific feedback.
  • As learning: Exit Card (self-assessment against entry goals), Unit 6 Discussion (figure-skating vectors: which component matters for a safe landing?).
  • Of learning: Summative Assignment (16 Q, 100 pts, hybrid auto/teacher-marked), Summative Test (26 Q, 100 pts, 120 min, hybrid).

Lesson sequence

  1. Entry Card → Lesson 6.1 (geometric vectors: magnitude and direction) — what a vector is.
  2. Lesson 6.2 (addition/subtraction: triangle and parallelogram laws) with resultant-magnitude work → Formative Check A (6.1 + 6.2).
  3. Lesson 6.3 (scalar multiplication, collinearity, unit vectors; 6.4 laws folded in).
  4. Lesson 6.4 (algebraic vectors in R² and R³: position vectors, component form) → Formative Check B (6.3 + 6.4 + 6.5).
  5. Lesson 6.5 (operations with algebraic vectors: componentwise arithmetic in R² and R³).
  6. Lesson 6.6 (linear combinations, spanning sets, coplanarity; figure-skating application) → Formative Check C (6.6 + 6.7 + 6.8).
  7. Summative Assignment → Summative Test → Discussion → Exit Card.

Materials

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 Ch. 6 (PDF pp. ~272–349, verified); Desmos 2D calculator (embedded in each lesson); one Khan Academy video per lesson (all verified public); 12 original vector diagrams (figures/ch6/).

Differentiation

Formative checks give instant targeted feedback for self-pacing. Extension: textbook-style "Think" problems (e.g. proving GP + GQ + GR = 0 for triangle medians; the coplanarity investigation Q13–Q17 bank in nelson-ch6.md) and the figure-skating Career Link investigation. Support: Desmos explorations make the parallelogram law, scalar multiplication, and linear combinations visual before the algebra; the "head minus tail" and "solve two equations, check the third" mantras give repeatable scaffolds for component and span problems.
