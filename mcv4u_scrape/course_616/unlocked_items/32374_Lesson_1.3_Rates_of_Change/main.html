Rates of Change

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §1.3 — velocity is the model; the same limit idea measures how fast anything changes.

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Compute an average rate of change (average velocity) over an interval.
  • Find an instantaneous rate of change (instantaneous velocity) as the limit of average rates over shrinking intervals.
  • Interpret the sign of a velocity and distinguish speed from velocity.

💡 Minds On

Many practical relationships involve interdependent quantities: a balloon's volume varies with its height, air temperature varies with elevation, a sphere's surface area varies with its radius. We often want to know how rapidly the dependent variable changes when the independent variable changes — that is the rate of change. In this section you will see that an instantaneous rate of change is found exactly the way you found a tangent slope: as the limit of a difference quotient.

Velocity as a rate of change

An object moving in a straight line is the classic rate-of-change model. Movement to the right (or up) is the positive direction; movement to the left (or down) is negative. A vehicle travelling 340 km north in 4 h has average velocity 340/4 = 85 km/h, since average velocity = (change in position)/(change in time). If s(t) gives the position at time t, the average rate of change of position over a time interval is average velocity = Δs/Δt.

Example 1 — A pebble is dropped from a cliff 80 m high. After t seconds, the pebble is s metres above the ground, where s(t) = 80 − 5t², 0 ≤ t ≤ 4.
a. Average velocity between t = 1 s and t = 3 s.   b. Average velocity between t = 1 s and t = 1.5 s.   c. Explain why the answers differ.

Solution.
a. average velocity = Δs/Δt; s(1) = 75; s(3) = 35.
average velocity = [s(3) − s(1)]/(3 − 1) = (35 − 75)/2 = (−40)/2 = −20 m/s.
The average velocity in this 2 s interval is −20 m/s.
b. s(1.5) = 80 − 5(1.5)² = 68.75.
average velocity = [s(1.5) − s(1)]/(1.5 − 1) = (68.75 − 75)/0.5 = −12.5 m/s.
The average velocity in this 0.5 s interval is −12.5 m/s.
c. Gravity makes the velocity grow with time, so the shorter 0.5 s interval gives a smaller average velocity — and a value closer to the actual velocity at t = 1.

Shorter and shorter intervals give −10.5, −10.05, −10.005 m/s — approaching −10 m/s. Algebraically, the average velocity over 1 ≤ t ≤ 1 + h is [s(1 + h) − s(1)]/h = [80 − 5(1 + h)² − 75]/h = (−10h − 5h²)/h = −10 − 5h (h ≠ 0). For a very short interval, 5h is close to 0, so the average velocity is close to −10 m/s. The instantaneous velocity at t = 1 is the limiting value as h → 0: v = limh→0(−10 − 5h) = −10 m/s.

In general, for position s(t), the average velocity from t = a to t = a + h is Δs/Δt = [s(a + h) − s(a)]/h — the slope of the secant PQ where P(a, s(a)) and Q(a + h, s(a + h)). The velocity at t = a is the limiting value of the average velocity as h → 0.

Instantaneous Velocity. The velocity of an object with position function s(t), at time t = a, is v(a) = limΔt→0 Δs/Δt = limh→0 [s(a + h) − s(a)]/h.
Note: v(a) is the slope of the tangent to the graph of s(t) at P(a, s(a)). The speed of an object is the absolute value of its velocity — speed says how fast, velocity says how fast and in which direction.

Example 2 — A toy rocket's height is s(t) = −5t² + 30t + 2 metres at t seconds. What is its velocity at t = 4?

Solution. s(4 + h) = −5(4 + h)² + 30(4 + h) + 2 = −80 − 40h − 5h² + 120 + 30h + 2 = −5h² − 10h + 42; s(4) = −5(4)² + 30(4) + 2 = 42.
v(4) = limh→0 [s(4 + h) − s(4)]/h   (substitute)
= limh→0 [−10h − 5h²]/h   (factor)
= limh→0 h(−10 − 5h)/h   (simplify)
= limh→0 (−10 − 5h)   (evaluate)
= −10.
Therefore, the velocity of the rocket is 10 m/s downward at t = 4 s.

Comparing average and instantaneous rates of change

Velocity is only one example. In general, if y depends on x by y = f(x), then as x changes from a to a + h (Δx = h), the change in y is Δy = f(a + h) − f(a).

Average Rate of Change. The difference quotient Δy/Δx = [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h is called the average rate of change in y with respect to x over the interval from x = a to x = a + h.

The average rate of change equals the slope of the secant PQ where P(a, f(a)) and Q(a + h, f(a + h)). The instantaneous rate of change in y with respect to x at x = a is the limiting value of the average rate of change as h → 0.

Instantaneous Rate of Change. The instantaneous rate of change in y = f(x) with respect to x at x = a is limΔx→0 Δy/Δx = limh→0 [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h, provided the limit exists.

As with velocity, the instantaneous rate of change at x = a equals the slope of the tangent to y = f(x) at x = a.

Example 3 — Manufacturing x calculators costs C(x) = 10√x + 1000 dollars.
a. What is the total cost of manufacturing 100 calculators?   b. What is the rate of change of the total cost with respect to x when x = 100?

Solution.
a. C(100) = 10√100 + 1000 = 1100. The total cost of 100 calculators is $1100.
b. The rate of change at x = 100 is
limh→0 [C(100 + h) − C(100)]/h   (substitute)
= limh→0 [10√(100 + h) + 1000 − 1100]/h
= limh→0 [10√(100 + h) − 100]/h × [10√(100 + h) + 100]/[10√(100 + h) + 100]   (rationalize the numerator)
= limh→0 [100(100 + h) − 10000]/[h(10√(100 + h) + 100)]   (expand)
= limh→0 100h/[h(10√(100 + h) + 100)]   (simplify)
= limh→0 100/[10√(100 + h) + 100]   (evaluate)
= 100/(10√(100 + 0) + 100) = 0.5.
Therefore, when 100 calculators are being produced, the total cost is changing at $0.50 per calculator.

An alternative form. The velocity at t = 1 can also be found from the interval 1 ≤ t ≤ t (a general time) by letting t → 1: v(1) = limt→1 [s(t) − s(1)]/(t − 1). In general, v(a) = limt→a [s(t) − s(a)]/(t − a), and the instantaneous rate of change of y = f(x) at x = a is limx→a [f(x) − f(a)]/(x − a).

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 1.3.

Question 1 — A bolt is dropped from 320 m: s(t) = 320 − 5t². Find the average velocity during the first second.

s(0) = 320, s(1) = 315. Average velocity = (315 − 320)/(1 − 0) = −5 m/s (5 m/s downward).

Question 2 — For the same bolt, find the velocity at t = 2.

v(2) = limh→0 [s(2+h) − s(2)]/h = limh→0 (−20h − 5h²)/h = −20 m/s (20 m/s downward).

Question 3 — Give a geometric interpretation of [s(9) − s(2)]/7 and of limh→0 [s(6 + h) − s(6)]/h.

The first is the slope of the secant joining the position graph at t = 2 and t = 9 (average velocity). The second is the slope of the tangent at t = 6 (instantaneous velocity).

Question 4 — What is wrong with the statement "The cheetah's speed was 65 km/h north"?

Speed has no direction — "65 km/h north" describes a velocity, not a speed.

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • Average velocity is found just like a secant slope: average velocity = (change in position)/(change in time).
  • Instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent to the position graph — found just like a tangent slope, as the limit of average velocities.

Need to know

  • From t = a to t = a + h, use the difference quotient with the position function s(t): Δs/Δt = [s(a + h) − s(a)]/h.
  • The velocity at t = a is the limiting value as h → 0: v(a) = limh→0 [s(a + h) − s(a)]/h.
  • Speed = |velocity| (magnitude only); velocity includes direction.
