Unit 3 Lesson Plan — Derivatives and Their Applications

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 3 — for teacher reference.

Course: MCV4U, Grade 12 Calculus and Vectors  |  Unit: 3 — Derivatives and Their Applications (Nelson Ch. 3)  |  Time: 12–14 periods (~75 min each)

Curriculum expectations (Strand B — Derivatives and Their Applications)

  • B1.1 — Determine second derivatives; connect position, velocity, and acceleration to first and second derivatives (3.1).
  • B1.2 — Solve problems involving rates of change in motion contexts (3.1).
  • B2.1 — Determine the extreme (absolute maximum/minimum) values of a function on a closed interval (3.2).
  • B2.2 — Build one-variable mathematical models, apply the extreme-value algorithm, and interpret optimization results in context (3.3).
  • B2.3 — Determine maximum and minimum values of models in economics (revenue, profit, cost) and science, respecting practical domain constraints (3.4).

Learning goals

Students will compute second derivatives with correct notation; model straight-line motion with s(t), v(t) = s′(t), a(t) = s″(t) and interpret signs; apply the extreme-value algorithm to find absolute maxima and minima on closed intervals; solve area/volume/distance optimization problems with diagrams and justified domains; and use marginal cost, revenue, and profit to optimize business and science models.

Success criteria

  • I can differentiate twice and write the result in f″(x), y″, and d²y/dx² notation.
  • I can state when an object is at rest (v = 0), and whether it is speeding up (v and a same sign) or slowing down (opposite signs).
  • I can run the extreme-value algorithm completely: critical points, endpoints, then compare — never skipping the endpoints.
  • I can turn a word problem into a one-variable function with a labelled diagram and a domain justified by the situation.
  • I can explain what marginal cost/revenue/profit mean in plain language and what P′ = 0 tells a decision-maker.

Assessment plan

  • For learning: Entry Card (feelings, goals, word-problem confidence), Formative Checks A/B/C with answer-specific feedback.
  • As learning: Exit Card (self-assessment against entry goals), Unit 3 Discussion (peer explanation of marginal reasoning).
  • Of learning: Summative Assignment (16 Q, 100 pts, hybrid auto/teacher-marked), Summative Test (26 Q, 100 pts, 120 min, hybrid).

Lesson sequence

  1. Entry Card → Lesson 3.1 (second derivatives: notation and computation) — the new differentiation skill.
  2. Lesson 3.2 (velocity and acceleration) with motion-graph reading → Formative Check A (3.1a + 3.1b).
  3. Lesson 3.3 (absolute max/min on an interval) — the extreme-value algorithm.
  4. Lesson 3.4 (optimization: area, volume, distance) with setup diagrams → Formative Check B (3.2 + 3.3).
  5. Lesson 3.5 (maximizing revenue and profit) — marginal analysis.
  6. Lesson 3.6 (minimizing cost in business and science) → Formative Check C (3.4 + mixed).
  7. Summative Assignment → Summative Test → Discussion → Exit Card.

Materials

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 Ch. 3; Desmos (embedded in each lesson); curated video per lesson; textbook figures embedded in lessons.

Differentiation

Formative checks give instant targeted feedback for self-pacing. Extension: textbook "Create"/"Think" problems (e.g. 3.1 Q14–Q18, 3.3 Q17–Q18, 3.4 Q16–Q19) and the Career Link mine-trucking investigation. Support: Desmos explorations make velocity/acceleration and the optimization peak visual before the algebra; the five-step optimization algorithm gives a repeatable scaffold for word problems.
