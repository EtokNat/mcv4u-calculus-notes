LESSON 8.5

Projections in Applications

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §7.5 (applied) — resolving vectors along ramps, ropes, and headings to find what actually acts in a chosen direction.

Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Resolve a physical vector into components along and perpendicular to a direction of interest.
  • Apply projections to ramps (how much of the weight acts down the slope?) and ropes (how much of the pull is useful?).
  • Find the component of one velocity along another direction (headwind/tailwind along a heading).

Minds On

A sled rope angled upward: how much of the pull actually drags the sled forward, and how much just lifts? A crate on a loading ramp: how much of its weight threatens to slide it down? A crosswind on a runway: how much of it becomes headwind along the runway? Each question asks for the projection of one vector onto a direction.

Learn — step by step

Weight on a ramp, resolved

Gravity pulls straight down, but a ramp constrains motion to its surface. Projecting the weight vector onto the ramp direction gives the sliding component (|W|sinθ down the slope); projecting onto the perpendicular gives the pressing component (|W|cosθ into the ramp), which determines friction and the ramp's required strength.

Example 1 — A 10 kg crate (weight 98 N) rests on a loading ramp inclined at 30°. Resolve the weight into components parallel and perpendicular to the ramp, and interpret each.

Solution. Parallel (down the ramp): 98·sin30° = 49 N — this is the force trying to slide the crate; friction or a brake must oppose it.
Perpendicular (into the ramp): 98·cos30° ≈ 84.9 N — this presses the crate into the surface and sets the ramp's load.
Check: 49² + 84.9² ≈ 2401 + 7208 = 9609 ≈ 98² ✓.

The useful part of a pull

When a rope pulls at an angle above the direction of motion, the useful component is the projection of the pull onto the motion direction: |F|cosφ. The rest lifts — sometimes helpfully (less friction), sometimes not.

Example 2 — A child pulls a sled with 200 N on a rope angled 20° above the snow. What is the forward (useful) component and the upward (lifting) component?

Solution. Forward: 200cos20° ≈ 187.9 N. Upward: 200sin20° ≈ 68.4 N.
The lift lightens the sled's contact with the snow (reducing friction) — but if the rope angle grew too large, the forward component would shrink and pulling would get harder.

Wind along the heading

For a pilot, only the wind's component along the heading changes ground speed (headwind slows, tailwind speeds); the perpendicular component causes drift. Projecting the wind vector onto the heading direction separates the two effects cleanly.

Example 3 — A plane heads due north at 400 km/h airspeed. The wind blows from the northeast at 100 km/h (so its velocity vector points southwest: 100(cos225°, sin225°)). What is the wind's component along the heading — i.e. how much headwind or tailwind does the plane feel?

Solution. Heading unit vector: (0, 1). Wind vector: (−70.71, −70.71).
Scalar projection of wind onto heading: (−70.71)(0) + (−70.71)(1) = −70.7 km/h.
The negative sign means a 70.7 km/h headwind — the plane's northward ground speed drops to 400 − 70.7 ≈ 329.3 km/h (before accounting for the sideways drift component of −70.7 km/h westward).

Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Applied projection problems.

Question 1 — A mover pushes a crate with 350 N at 40° below the horizontal. What is the forward (useful) component?

350cos40° ≈ 350(0.7660) ≈ 268.1 N.

Question 2 — At what ramp angle does the sliding component of a crate's weight equal the component pressing it into the ramp?

|W|sinθ = |W|cosθ → tanθ = 1 → θ = 45°.

Question 3 — A rope pulls a wagon with 120 N at 35° above the horizontal. Find the forward and upward components. Which one does the work?

Forward: 120cos35° ≈ 98.3 N; upward: 120sin35° ≈ 68.8 N. Only the forward component does work, since only it lies along the displacement.

Question 4 — The projection of vector a onto vector b is negative. What does that tell you about the angle between them?

The scalar projection equals |a|cosθ, so a negative projection means cosθ < 0 — the angle is obtuse (90° < θ ≤ 180°): a points partly against b's direction.

Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • A projection answers "how much of this vector acts in that direction?"
  • On a ramp: weight splits into |W|sinθ down the slope and |W|cosθ into the slope.
  • Only the component along the motion does work or changes speed; perpendicular components cause drift or pressing.

Need to know

  • A negative projection means the vector points partly opposite the chosen direction (obtuse angle).
