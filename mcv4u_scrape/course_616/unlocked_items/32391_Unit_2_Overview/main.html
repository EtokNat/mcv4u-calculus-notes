Unit 2 — Derivatives

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 2 (pp. 61–114) — the rules that make differentiation fast.

Textbook: Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 2 (pp. 61–114). Strands A/B — Rate of Change; Derivatives and Their Applications. Suggested pacing: 12–14 periods.

In Unit 1 you found tangent slopes one painful limit at a time. This unit builds the shortcut rules — constant, power, constant multiple, sum/difference, product, quotient, and chain — that turn differentiation into algebra. Along the way you'll meet the derivative function, learn exactly where derivatives can fail to exist, and see why a business pricing its products needs an instantaneous rate of change, not an average.

Ontario curriculum expectations (Chapter 2)

  • Understand and determine derivatives of polynomial and simple rational functions from first principles (§2.1).
  • Identify examples of functions that are not differentiable (§2.1).
  • Justify and use the rules for determining derivatives (§2.2, §2.3, §2.4, §2.5).
  • Identify composition as two functions applied in succession; determine the composition of two functions expressed in notation; decompose a given composite function into its parts (§2.5).
  • Use the derivative to solve problems involving instantaneous rates of change (§2.2–§2.5).

Learning goals

  • I can find f′(a) and f′(x) from the limit definition and explain what the derivative function represents.
  • I can identify where a function fails to be differentiable (cusp, vertical tangent, discontinuity, corner).
  • I can differentiate polynomials and simple rational/radical functions using the constant, power, constant multiple, sum, and difference rules.
  • I can apply the product rule, the quotient rule, and the chain rule — and choose the right rule for each function.
  • I can find equations of tangents and normals and interpret derivatives as instantaneous rates of change in context.

How you will be assessed

  • Formative Checks A, B, C — practice quizzes with feedback on every answer (for learning, 0 points).
  • Unit 2 Summative Assignment — 16 questions, 100 points (of learning).
  • Unit 2 Summative Test — 26 questions, 100 points, 120-minute limit (of learning).
