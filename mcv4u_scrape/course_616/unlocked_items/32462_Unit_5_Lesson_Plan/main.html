UNIT 5 LESSON PLAN

Derivatives of Exponential and Trigonometric Functions

Course: MCV4U, Grade 12 Calculus and Vectors  |  Unit: 5 — Derivatives of Exponential and Trigonometric Functions (Nelson Ch. 5)  |  Time: 12–14 periods (~75 min each)

Curriculum expectations (Strand B — Derivatives and Their Applications)

  • B-expectation — Define the number e; determine the derivative of y = e^x and of e^(g(x)) via the chain rule (5.1).
  • B-expectation — Determine the derivative of y = b^x; derive the rule from the limit definition (lim(h→0)(b^h−1)/h = ln b); apply the chain-rule form to b^(g(x)) (5.2).
  • B-expectation — Compare the graph of an exponential function with the graph of its derivative; identify the stretch factor ln b (5.1, 5.2).
  • B-expectation — Solve optimization problems involving exponential functions with the extreme-value algorithm (5.3).
  • B-expectation — Determine d/dx(sin x) = cos x and d/dx(cos x) = −sin x (x in radians); apply with product/quotient/chain rules (5.4).
  • B-expectation — Determine d/dx(tan x) = sec²x from the sine/cosine rules; apply the chain-rule form (5.5).
  • B-expectation — Solve rate-of-change problems: exponential growth/decay (doubling time, half-life, rates), harmonic motion (velocity, acceleration, s″ + k²s = 0) (5.1–5.5).

🎯 Learning Goals

Students will differentiate e^x and general exponentials (with the chain rule), derive and apply d/dx(b^x) = b^x·ln b, solve exponential optimization problems, differentiate sine, cosine, and tangent (with all prior rules), and model growth/decay and harmonic motion with the Chapter 5 rules.

Success criteria

  • I can state d/dx(e^x) = e^x and d/dx(e^(g(x))) = e^(g(x))·g′(x), and explain why the power rule cannot differentiate e^x.
  • I can state d/dx(b^x) = b^x·ln b, derive it from the limit definition, and recover the e^x rule as the b = e special case.
  • I can run the extreme-value algorithm on exponential models, use the "e^u never zero" shortcut, and solve e^u = k with natural logs.
  • I can state the sine, cosine, and tangent rules (with the minus sign on cosine), apply them inside composites, and find tangents and extrema of sinusoidal functions.
  • I can find doubling times, half-lives, and rates for P(t) = P₀e^(rt), and velocity/acceleration for s(t) = A sin kt, verifying s″ + k²s = 0.

Assessment plan

  • For learning: Entry Card (prior knowledge: chain rule, radians; confidence; goals), Formative Checks A/B/C with answer-specific feedback.
  • As learning: Exit Card (self-assessment, what clicked, what needs review, summative readiness), Unit 5 Discussion (peer explanation of growth/decay reasoning).
  • Of learning: Summative Assignment (16 Q, 100 pts, hybrid auto/teacher-marked), Summative Test (26 Q, 100 pts, 120 min, hybrid).

Lesson sequence

  1. Entry Card → Lesson 5.1 (e^x and the chain rule) — the simplest rule in calculus.
  2. Lesson 5.2 (b^x and the ln b stretch factor) — growth vs decay → Formative Check A (5.1 + 5.2).
  3. Lesson 5.3 (exponential optimization) — study effectiveness, ad profit, drug timing.
  4. Lesson 5.4 (derivatives of sine and cosine) — slopes that trace the other curve → Formative Check B (5.3 + 5.4).
  5. Lesson 5.5 (derivative of tangent) — derived from sine and cosine; always increasing.
  6. Lesson 5.6 (capstone: growth, decay, harmonic motion) — P(t) = P₀e^(rt), doubling time, springs, s″ + k²s = 0 → Formative Check C (5.5 + 5.6).
  7. Summative Assignment → Summative Test → Discussion → Exit Card.

Materials

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 Ch. 5 (PDF pp. 221–272, section pages verified by direct PDF inspection); Desmos (embedded in each lesson); curated video per lesson; 13 figures (8 extracted from the textbook PDF, 5 original matplotlib) embedded in lessons.

Differentiation

Formative checks give instant targeted feedback for self-pacing. Extension: "Thinking" items (hyperbolic-function derivatives, logistic-model inflection, constructing models from doubling data, the y″ + k²y = 0 result for A cos kt + B sin kt). Support: every lesson's Desmos exploration builds the rule visually before the algebra; worked examples show every step; the optimization algorithm gives a repeatable scaffold.
