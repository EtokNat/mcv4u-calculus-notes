Geometric Vectors: Magnitude and Direction

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §6.1 — scalars vs. vectors, and the directed line segment.

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Distinguish a scalar (magnitude only) from a vector (magnitude and direction).
  • Represent a vector as a directed line segment and read off its magnitude |AB|.
  • Identify equal and opposite vectors and explain why a vector can be moved without changing it.

💡 Minds On

"The plane is doing 500 km/h" sounds complete — until you have to land it. 500 km/h tells you how fast, but not where it's going. Speed is a single number; velocity needs a number and a direction. That two-part quantity is a vector, and this whole unit is built on it.

🔍 Explore — arrows have two jobs

  1. In Desmos, plot the points (0,0) and (3,4). The arrow from the first to the second is a vector — its length is the magnitude and its pointing is the direction.
  2. The distance between the points is sqrt((3-0)^2+(4-0)^2) = 5. Type sqrt(3^2+4^2) to confirm: the magnitude is 5.
  3. Now plot (6,1) and (9,5). Same length, same direction — a different arrow, but the same vector. Conclusion: only length and direction define a vector; position does not.

📖 Learn — step by step

Key term — Vector: a quantity with both magnitude (size) and direction. A scalar has magnitude only. Speed is a scalar; velocity is a vector.
  • The directed line segment. Draw an arrow from point A (the tail) to point B (the head). The arrow AB is a vector: its length is the magnitude, written |AB|, and the arrowhead gives the direction. Magnitudes are never negative.
  • Equal vectors. Two vectors are equal (equivalent) if they have the same magnitude and the same direction — even if they sit in different places. Picking a vector up and moving it without turning or stretching it changes nothing. That's why the velocity of a plane over Calgary can equal the velocity of a plane over Toronto.
  • Opposite vectors. Two vectors with the same magnitude but opposite directions are opposites: BA = −AB and |AB| = |BA|. Vectors are often named with lowercase letters: v and −v are opposites.
  • Same magnitude is not enough. Two arrows of equal length pointing different ways are not equal vectors — direction is half the definition.

Example 1 — Rhombus ABCD has diagonals meeting at E. Name vectors equal to each of: (a) AB, (b) DA, (c) EB, (d) AE.

Solution. A rhombus is a parallelogram with four equal sides, so opposite sides are parallel and equal in length, and the diagonals bisect each other.
(a) AB = DC — same length, same direction.
(b) DA = CB — same length, same direction.
(c) EB = DE — the diagonals bisect each other.
(d) AE = EC — the diagonals bisect each other.
Note that AB ≠ DA: the magnitudes match, but the directions differ — equal magnitude alone never makes vectors equal.

Example 2 — Vector AB has tail (−4, 2) and head (−1, 3). (a) Find |AB|. (b) If C is (−6, 0) and CD = AB, find D.

Solution.
(a) |AB| = √((−1 − (−4))² + (3 − 2)²) = √(3² + 1²) = √10.
(b) Equal vectors have the same "step": from C(−6, 0), take the same step (+3 in x, +1 in y) to reach D = (−6 + 3, 0 + 1) = (−3, 1).

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 6.1.

Question 1 — Which are vectors and which are scalars: temperature, weight, distance, displacement, force, speed?

Vectors: weight, displacement, force (each needs a direction). Scalars: temperature, distance, speed (a single number says it all).

Question 2 — True or false: "If two vectors have the same magnitude, then they are equal." Justify.

False. Equal vectors need the same magnitude and the same direction — two equal-length arrows pointing different ways are not equal.

Question 3 — A car travels northeast at 70 km/h. Describe its opposite vector in words.

Same speed, reversed direction: a car travelling southwest at 70 km/h. If the first is v, the opposite is −v.

Question 4 — Explain why translating a vector (sliding it without turning) does not change the vector.

A vector is defined by magnitude and direction only. Sliding changes neither the arrow's length nor where it points, so the vector is identical — position is not part of the definition.

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • Vector = magnitude + direction (arrow); scalar = magnitude only (number).
  • AB: tail A → head B; |AB| is its (non-negative) magnitude.
  • Equal vectors: same magnitude, same direction — position is irrelevant. Opposite vectors: same magnitude, reversed direction (BA = −AB).

Need to know

  • Equal length alone never makes vectors equal — check the direction too.
