Derivatives of Sine and Cosine

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §5.4 — discovering d/dx(sin x) = cos x and d/dx(cos x) = −sin x, then combining them with every rule you already know.

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • State d/dx(sin x) = cos x and d/dx(cos x) = −sin x, and explain the numerical discovery behind them.
  • Apply the chain rule to sin(g(x)) and cos(g(x)), combined with product and quotient rules.
  • Find tangent lines and absolute extrema of sinusoidal functions.
  • Explain why angles must be in radians for these rules to hold.

💡 Minds On

Here is the whole surprise of this lesson: the slope of a sine wave traces out a cosine wave. At x = 0, sin x is climbing at its steepest — slope 1 — and cos 0 = 1. At x = π/2, sine is flat on top — slope 0 — and cos(π/2) = 0. At x = π, sine is falling at its steepest — slope −1 — and cos π = −1. The derivative of sine is cosine, point for point. The derivative of cosine is nearly as tidy — with one minus sign that catches everyone at least once.

📖 Learn — step by step

The two rules

Tabulating d/dx(sin x) at x = 0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, … and comparing with cos x shows the columns match; the same experiment for d/dx(cos x) matches −sin x. These identities only hold when x is in radians — the natural unit for calculus.

RULES — Sine and cosine
d/dx(sin x) = cos x      d/dx(cos x) = −sin x
Chain-rule forms: d/dx(sin f(x)) = cos f(x)·f′(x);   d/dx(cos f(x)) = −sin f(x)·f′(x).

Example 1 — Differentiate (a) y = cos 3x and (b) y = x sin x.

Solution.
(a) Chain rule: y′ = −3 sin 3x.

(b) Product rule: y′ = (1)(sin x) + (x)(cos x) = sin x + x cos x.

Chain-rule combinations (and sin²x means (sin x)²)

Example 2 — Differentiate (a) y = sin(x²), (b) y = sin²x, and (c) y = cos(1 + x³).

Solution.
(a) y′ = cos(x²)·(2x) = 2x cos(x²).

(b) Think of (sin x)²: power rule on the outside, then the sine rule inside: y′ = 2 sin x·cos x (which equals sin 2x).

(c) y′ = −sin(1 + x³)·(3x²) = −3x² sin(1 + x³).

Mixed models and extrema

Exponentials and trig functions mix freely — and the same critical-number machinery finds tangents and extrema of wavy curves.

Example 3 — Determine the equation of the tangent to y = x cos 2x at x = π/2.

Solution. Point: (π/2, (π/2)·cos π) = (π/2, −π/2).

y′ = (1)(cos 2x) + (x)(−2 sin 2x) = cos 2x − 2x sin 2x. At x = π/2: y′ = cos π − π·sin π = −1 − 0 = −1.

Tangent: y + π/2 = −1(x − π/2), which simplifies to y = −x.

For extremes, differentiate and solve: for f(x) = cos²x on [0, 2π], f′(x) = −2 sin x cos x = 0 gives x = 0, π/2, π, 3π/2, 2π — and the graph shows maxima of 1 at 0, π, 2π and minima of 0 at π/2, 3π/2, exactly as cosine's range predicts.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 5.4.

Question 1 — Differentiate y = sin 5x.

Chain rule: y′ = 5 cos 5x.

Question 2 — Differentiate y = cos(x² + 1).

y′ = −sin(x² + 1)·(2x) = −2x sin(x² + 1).

Question 3 — True or false: "d²/dx²(sin x) = sin x." Justify.

False. Differentiating twice: d/dx(sin x) = cos x, then d/dx(cos x) = −sin x. (Two derivatives return the negative of the original — a fact springs exploit in Lesson 5.6.)

Question 4 — Find the equation of the tangent to y = sin x at x = π/6.

Point: (π/6, 1/2). Slope: y′ = cos x, so y′(π/6) = √3/2. Tangent: y − 1/2 = (√3/2)(x − π/6).

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • d/dx(sin x) = cos x and d/dx(cos x) = −sin x (x in radians).
  • Chain-rule forms: d/dx(sin f(x)) = cos f(x)·f′(x); d/dx(cos f(x)) = −sin f(x)·f′(x).
  • Combine with product, quotient, and chain rules — and with exponentials, as in esin x + cos x.
  • sin²x means (sin x)² — use the power rule on the outside.

Need to know

  • The minus sign in d/dx(cos x) is the #1 slip — double-check it on every problem.
