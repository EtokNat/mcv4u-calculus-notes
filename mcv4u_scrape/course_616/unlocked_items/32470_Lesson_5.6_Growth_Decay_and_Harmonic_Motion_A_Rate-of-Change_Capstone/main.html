💡 Minds On

Two facts you already know, side by side: (1) the derivative of ex is a copy of ex, and (2) the second derivative of sin x is −sin x. The first fact says exponential growth feeds on itself — more bacteria means faster growth, which means more bacteria. The second says a spring's acceleration always points back toward the middle — which is why it oscillates instead of running away. This lesson turns both facts into working models.

📖 Learn — step by step

Exponential models: the rate is proportional to the amount

For P(t) = P₀ert, the derivative is P′(t) = rP₀ert = r·P(t) — the rate of change is always the same fraction r of the current amount. If r > 0 the model grows (doubling time t = ln 2 / r); if r < 0 it decays (half-life t = ln 2 / |r|).

Example 1 — A bacteria colony follows P(t) = 500e0.1t (t in hours). Find the rate-of-change function, the growth rates after 1 h and 8 h, the doubling time, and the time when the growth rate reaches 500 bacteria/h.

Solution. P′(t) = 50e0.1t bacteria/h. P′(1) ≈ 55/h; P′(8) ≈ 111/h — the rate itself is growing exponentially.

Doubling: 500e0.1t = 1000 → e0.1t = 2 → 0.1t = ln 2 → t ≈ 6.93 h.

P′(t) = 500 → 50e0.1t = 500 → e0.1t = 10 → 0.1t = ln 10 → t ≈ 23.0 h.

Example 2 — A radioactive substance decays as A(t) = 100e−0.3t (A in percent, t in years). Find the rate-of-decay function and the rate when half the sample is gone.

Solution. A′(t) = −30e−0.3t %/year — the negative sign marks decay.

Half gone: 100e−0.3t = 50 → t = (ln 2)/0.3 ≈ 2.31 years. Then A′(2.31) = −30e−0.3(2.31) = −30(0.5) = −15 %/year — at the half-life the sample is losing 15% of the original per year.

Harmonic motion: velocity and acceleration from s(t)

For a mass on a spring, position s(t) = A sin kt gives velocity v = s′ = Ak cos kt and acceleration a = v′ = −Ak² sin kt. Notice a = −k²·s(t): acceleration is the negative of position (times k²) — always pulling back toward equilibrium.

Example 3 — A spring's displacement is s(t) = 8 sin(10πt) cm (t in seconds). Find the velocity and acceleration functions, verify that s″ + 100π²s = 0, and find the maximum speed.

Solution. v(t) = s′(t) = 8·10π·cos(10πt) = 80π cos(10πt) cm/s.

a(t) = v′(t) = −80π·10π·sin(10πt) = −800π² sin(10πt) cm/s². Since s(t) = 8 sin(10πt), a(t) = −100π²·s(t), so s″ + 100π²s = 0 ✓.

Maximum speed: |80π cos(10πt)| ≤ 80π, so vmax = 80π ≈ 251 cm/s, occurring whenever the spring passes through s = 0.

RULE — The hallmark of simple harmonic motion
If s(t) = A cos kt + B sin kt, then s″(t) = −k²s(t), i.e. s″ + k²s = 0. Whenever you see a second derivative equal to a negative constant times the function, you are looking at oscillation.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Drawn from the chapter's applications.

Question 1 — For P(t) = 200e0.05t, find the doubling time.

200e0.05t = 400 → e0.05t = 2 → t = (ln 2)/0.05 ≈ 13.86 time units.

Question 2 — A particle has velocity v(t) = 10e−0.5t cm/s. Find its acceleration and explain what it means.

a(t) = v′(t) = −5e−0.5t = −0.5·v(t) cm/s². The acceleration is always half the velocity in the backward direction — the particle slows down, approaching rest exponentially.

Question 3 — For s(t) = 8 sin 2t, find v(t) and the maximum speed.

v(t) = 16 cos 2t. Maximum speed = 16, occurring when s = 0 (passing through equilibrium).

Question 4 — True or false: "A decaying exponential's rate of change shrinks in magnitude over time." Justify.

True. If P(t) = P₀e−kt with k > 0, then |P′(t)| = kP₀e−kt — an exponential that shrinks toward zero as t grows.

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • P(t) = P₀ert ⇒ P′ = rP — rate proportional to amount. Doubling time = ln 2 / r; half-life = ln 2 / |r|.
  • Harmonic motion: s(t) = A sin kt ⇒ v = Ak cos kt, a = −Ak² sin kt; max speed occurs at s = 0.
  • s″ + k²s = 0 is the signature equation of simple harmonic motion.
  • Every rule in Chapter 5 (ex, bx, sin/cos, tan) combines with product, quotient, and chain rules.

Need to know

  • Watch units and signs: growth rates are positive, decay rates negative; velocity is signed, speed is not.
