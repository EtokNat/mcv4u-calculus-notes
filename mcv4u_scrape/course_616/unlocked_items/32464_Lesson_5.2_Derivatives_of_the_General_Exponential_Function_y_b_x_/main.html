Derivatives of the General Exponential Function (y = bx)

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §5.2 — why the derivative of bx is a stretched copy of bx, and where the constant ln b comes from.

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Explain why f′(x)/f(x) is constant for f(x) = bx, and identify that constant as ln b.
  • Use d/dx(bx) = bx·ln b and the chain-rule form d/dx(bg(x)) = bg(x)·ln b·g′(x).
  • Recover the ex rule as a special case (since ln e = 1).
  • Model exponential growth and decay (population, radioactivity) and interpret the sign of the derivative.

💡 Minds On

We now know d/dx(ex) = ex. But what about 2x, 10x, or 5x? Imagine graphing f(x) = 2x and its derivative side by side: the derivative has exactly the same shape — just vertically squashed (about 0.69 times as tall). For 3x, the derivative is the same shape but stretched (about 1.10 times as tall). There is a special constant hiding in each base that controls the stretch. This lesson finds it.

📖 Learn — step by step

The derivative is a stretched copy of the original

Tabulating f(x) = 2x and its numerical derivative shows a striking pattern: the ratio f′(x)/f(x) ≈ 0.69 at every x. So f′(x) ≈ 0.69·2x — the derivative is a vertically compressed copy of the original. For f(x) = 3x the same ratio is ≈ 1.10, so f′(x) ≈ 1.10·3x — a vertically stretched copy.

Where the constant comes from

Start from the definition of the derivative:
f′(x) = limh→0 [bx+h − bx]/h = bx · limh→0 (bh − 1)/h.
The limit in parentheses does not involve x — it is a constant determined by the base b alone. That constant is exactly ln b: for b = 2 it equals ln 2 ≈ 0.693147; for b = 3 it equals ln 3 ≈ 1.098612 — matching the investigation above.

RULE — Derivative of bx
If f(x) = bx (b > 0, b ≠ 1), then f′(x) = bx · ln b.
Chain-rule form: if f(x) = bg(x), then f′(x) = bg(x) · ln b · g′(x).
Check: for b = e, ln e = 1 and the rule reduces to d/dx(ex) = ex ✓.

Example 1 — Determine the derivative of (a) f(x) = 5x and (b) g(x) = 53x−2.

Solution.
(a) f′(x) = (ln 5)·5x.

(b) Chain rule: g′(x) = 53x−2 · ln 5 · (3) = 3·53x−2·ln 5.

Growth vs decay: read the sign

When 0 < b < 1, the function decreases, and ln b < 0 — so the derivative bx·ln b is negative, as it should be. The sign of ln b always matches the direction of change.

Example 2 — The population of the town of Goldrushtown, t years after 1850, is P(t) = 50,000(0.98)t. Find the population after 50 years and the rate of change then.

Solution. P(50) = 50,000(0.98)50 ≈ 18,208 people.

P′(t) = 50,000(0.98)t·ln(0.98), so P′(50) ≈ −367.86 people/year. The negative sign means the population is decreasing by about 368 people per year at t = 50 — exactly what the falling graph shows.

Example 3 — Determine the equation of the tangent to y = 10x at (1, 10).

Solution. y′ = 10x·ln 10, so the slope at x = 1 is 10·ln 10 ≈ 23.03.
Tangent: y − 10 = (10 ln 10)(x − 1), or y = (10 ln 10)x + 10 − 10 ln 10.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 5.2.

Question 1 — Differentiate y = 24x.

Chain rule: y′ = 24x · ln 2 · 4 = 4·24x·ln 2.

Question 2 — For f(x) = 3x, evaluate f′(0).

f′(x) = 3x·ln 3, so f′(0) = 30·ln 3 = ln 3 ≈ 1.099 — matching the investigation's constant.

Question 3 — True or false: "d/dx(2x) = x·2x−1." Justify.

False. That is the power rule applied to a variable exponent — the same mistake as in Lesson 5.1. The correct rule is d/dx(2x) = 2x·ln 2.

Question 4 — A bacterial culture has N(t) = 100(1.5)t bacteria. Find N′(2) and interpret it.

N′(t) = 100(1.5)t·ln 1.5, so N′(2) = 100(2.25)(0.4055) ≈ 91 bacteria per hour: at t = 2 the culture is growing by about 91 bacteria each hour.

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • The derivative of bx is a vertically stretched copy of bx: d/dx(bx) = bx·ln b, and d/dx(bg(x)) = bg(x)·ln b·g′(x).
  • The stretch factor ln b comes from the limit definition: limh→0(bh − 1)/h = ln b.
  • For 0 < b < 1, ln b < 0 and the derivative is negative — exponential decay.
  • When b = e, ln e = 1 recovers Lesson 5.1's rule.

Need to know

  • ln 2 ≈ 0.6931 and ln 3 ≈ 1.0986 — the constants from the investigation are logarithms.
