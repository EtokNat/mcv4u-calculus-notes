Learning Goals

  • Compute volumes of boxes and crates with the scalar triple product (extension).

Learn — step by step

Distances from displacement vectors

The distance between two points is the magnitude of the displacement vector joining them. Surveyors and navigators work in coordinates: subtract to get the displacement, then take its length.

Example 1 — A surveyor records two boundary markers at A(2, 3, 7) and B(0, −3, 4) in metres. What is the straight-line distance between them?

Solution. Displacement AB = B − A = (−2, −6, −3).
Distance = |AB| = √(4 + 36 + 9) = √49 = 7 m.

Areas: parallelogram and triangular plots

For a plot with sides given by vectors a and b: parallelogram area = |a × b| and triangle area = ½|a × b|. In two dimensions the cross-product magnitude is just |a_x·b_y − a_y·b_x| — the determinant of the side vectors.

Example 2 — A parallelogram-shaped field has one side 75 m long and an adjacent side 40 m long, meeting at 60°. What is its area?

Solution. Take a = (75, 0) and b = 40(cos60°, sin60°) = (20, 34.64).
Area = |a × b| = |75(34.64) − 0(20)| ≈ 2598 m².
Equivalently: 75 × 40 × sin60° ≈ 2598 m².

Example 3 — A triangular building lot has corners at A(0, 0), B(120, 30), and C(40, 100) in metres. What is its area?

Solution. AB = (120, 30), AC = (40, 100).
Area = ½|AB × AC| = ½|120(100) − 30(40)| = ½|12000 − 1200| = ½(10800) = 5400 m².

Volumes: the scalar triple product (extension)

The volume of the box (parallelepiped) spanned by three edge vectors u, v, w is the absolute value of the scalar triple product: V = |u · (v × w)|. For a rectangular box this reduces to length × width × height — but the triple product handles any slanted crate.

Example 4 — A shipping crate has edges u = (4, 0, 0), v = (0, 3, 0), and w = (0, 0, 2.5) in metres. What volume of cargo can it hold?

Solution. v × w = (3(2.5) − 0(0), 0(0) − 0(2.5), 0(0) − 3(0)) = (7.5, 0, 0).
V = |u · (v × w)| = |(4, 0, 0) · (7.5, 0, 0)| = |30| = 30 m³ — matching 4 × 3 × 2.5, as expected for a rectangular crate.

Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Applied geometry problems.

Question 1 — Two survey stakes are at P(1, −2, 5) and Q(4, 2, 5) in metres. How far apart are they?

PQ = (3, 4, 0); |PQ| = √(9 + 16) = 5 m.

Question 2 — A triangular garden has vertices (0, 0), (9, 2), and (3, 8) in metres. Find its area.

½|(9, 2) × (3, 8)| = ½|9(8) − 2(3)| = ½|72 − 6| = ½(66) = 33 m².

Question 3 — A parallelogram billboard frame is spanned by a = (6, 1, 0) and b = (2, 5, 0) in metres. What area of poster fits it?

|a × b| = |6(5) − 1(2)| = |30 − 2| = 28 m².

Question 4 — A storage box has edges (2, 0, 0), (0, 2, 0), and (0, 0, 3) in metres. Use the scalar triple product to find its volume.

(0, 2, 0) × (0, 0, 3) = (6, 0, 0); V = |(2, 0, 0) · (6, 0, 0)| = 12 m³.

Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • Distance between points = magnitude of the displacement vector.
  • Area: parallelogram = |a × b|; triangle = ½|a × b|.
  • Volume: parallelepiped = |u · (v × w)|, the scalar triple product.

Need to know

  • In 2D, |a × b| = |a_x·b_y − a_y·b_x| — no 3D machinery needed for flat plots.
  • The triple product is zero exactly when the three edges are coplanar (flat box, zero volume).
