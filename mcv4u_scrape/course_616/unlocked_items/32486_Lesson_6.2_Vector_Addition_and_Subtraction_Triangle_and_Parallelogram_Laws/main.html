🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Add vectors with the parallelogram law (tail-to-tail → diagonal) and the triangle law (head-to-tail).
  • Subtract vectors as a + (−b) and read a − b off a tail-to-tail diagram.
  • Find a resultant's magnitude with the cosine law and describe its direction.

💡 Minds On

Two tugboats pull a stuck ship — one left, one right. The ship doesn't follow either rope; it follows the combination of both pulls. Somewhere between the two directions, with a strength that is more than each pull but less than their sum. Today you learn the geometry that predicts exactly where the ship goes.

🔍 Explore — the sum is a diagonal

  1. In Desmos, plot (0,0), (4,1), and (1,3). Draw arrows from the origin to each of the other two points — call them a and b.
  2. The parallelogram on a and b has its fourth corner at (5,4). The arrow from the origin to (5, 4) is a + b — the diagonal. Confirm: (4+1, 1+3) = (5, 4).
  3. Now find a − b: flip b to (-1,-3) and add. Conclusion: the diagonal you draw depends on how you arrange the arrows — tail-to-tail gives the sum as the long diagonal.

📖 Learn — step by step

Key term — Resultant: the single vector that has the same combined effect as two or more vectors added together.
  • Parallelogram law. Place a and b tail-to-tail and complete the parallelogram. The sum a + b is the diagonal starting at the common tail. This is the tugboat picture: each rope is a force, the diagonal is where the ship actually goes.
  • Triangle law. Place b's tail at a's head (head-to-tail). Then a + b is the arrow from a's tail to b's head. Walking A → B → C lands you where walking A → C directly lands you: AB + BC = AC.
  • Subtraction. a − b means a + (−b): flip b around, then add. Arranged tail-to-tail, a − b is the arrow from the head of b to the head of a — the vector you'd add to b to get a.
  • The zero vector. a + (−a) = 0: magnitude 0, no direction. A vector plus its opposite cancels completely.
  • How big is the sum? |a + b| ≤ |a| + |b| — the resultant is never longer than the two lengths laid end to end, and equals their sum only when a and b point the same way. For the exact length, use the cosine law in the addition triangle (careful: the triangle's interior angle is the supplement of the tail-to-tail angle).

Example 1 — Vectors a and b have |a| = 3, |b| = 2, and the angle between them (tail-to-tail) is 60°. Find |a + b|.

Solution. In the addition triangle, the angle opposite the resultant is the supplement: 180° − 60° = 120°. Cosine law:
|a + b|² = |a|² + |b|² − 2|a||b|cos 120°
= 9 + 4 − 2(3)(2)(−1/2)
= 13 + 6 = 19
|a + b| = √19 ≈ 4.36.
Note the result is less than 3 + 2 = 5 — pulling at an angle always "wastes" some effort.

Example 2 — An airplane heads due south at 300 km/h and meets a wind from the west at 100 km/h. Find the airplane's resultant (ground) velocity.

Solution. Let v be the plane's velocity (south, 300) and w the wind's velocity (east, 100). Placed head-to-tail they form a right triangle, so the ground velocity g = v + w is the hypotenuse:
|g|² = 300² + 100² = 100 000 → |g| = √100 000 ≈ 316.23 km/h.
For direction, tan θ = 100/300 = 1/3, so θ = tan⁻¹(1/3) ≈ 18.4°.
The plane travels at 316.23 km/h on a heading of S18.4°E — the wind pushed it off course and sped it up.

Example 3 — In a rectangular box, OA = a, OC = b, OD = c. Express BC, OB, and OF in terms of a, b, c.

Solution. Draw 2x and −y head-to-tail.
BC = −a — it runs opposite to a with the same length.
OB = a + b — the diagonal of rectangle OABC (parallelogram law).
OF = OB + BF = (a + b) + c = a + b + c — the box's long diagonal, adding the third edge.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 6.2.

Question 1 — In △ABC, write AB + BC as a single vector.

Head-to-tail: AB + BC = AC — the arrow from the first tail to the last head.

Question 2 — |x| = 7, |y| = 24, and the angle between x and y is 90°. Find |x + y|.

Right angle → Pythagoras: |x + y| = √(7² + 24²) = √625 = 25.

Question 3 — With a and b tail-to-tail, how do you read the vector a − b off the diagram?

The arrow from the head of b to the head of a — it's the vector you must add to b to reach a (equivalently, a + (−b)).

Question 4 — Two forces pull in exactly opposite directions. What is the magnitude of their sum?

The difference of the magnitudes: |a + b| = ||a| − |b|| — the pulls partly cancel, and if they're equal the resultant is the zero vector.

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • Parallelogram law (tail-to-tail): a + b is the diagonal. Triangle law (head-to-tail): a + b runs first-tail to last-head.
  • a − b = a + (−b); tail-to-tail it's the head-of-b → head-of-a arrow. a + (−a) = 0.
  • |a + b| ≤ |a| + |b|; exact lengths via the cosine law (use the supplement angle).

Need to know

  • A resultant needs both a magnitude and a direction (e.g. S18.4°E) — never report just the length.
