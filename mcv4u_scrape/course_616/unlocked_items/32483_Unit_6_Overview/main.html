UNIT 6 OVERVIEW

Vectors

Textbook: Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 6 — Introduction to Vectors (PDF pp. ~272–349, verified). Strand C — Geometry and Algebra of Vectors. Suggested pacing: 12–14 periods.

Calculus described change; vectors describe direction. This unit builds the full vector toolkit from the ground up: what makes a quantity a vector, how to add and subtract arrows with the triangle and parallelogram laws, how scalars stretch and flip them, how coordinates turn geometry into arithmetic in R² and R³, and how a handful of vectors can combine to build everything — linear combinations and spanning sets. The capstone application comes from the chapter's figure-skating career link: a jump, a push, and a braking force, all added as vectors.

Ontario curriculum expectations (Chapter 6)

  • Represent vectors as directed line segments; recognize a vector as a quantity with both magnitude and direction (6.1).
  • Perform mathematical operations on geometric vectors: addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication (6.2, 6.3).
  • Determine properties of vector operations: commutative, associative, distributive laws (6.4).
  • Determine the Cartesian representation of a vector in two- and three-dimensional space (6.5, 6.6, 6.7, 6.8).
  • Perform mathematical operations on algebraic vectors in two- and three-dimensional space (6.6, 6.7, 6.8).

🎯 Learning Goals

  • I can distinguish scalars from vectors and identify equal and opposite vectors.
  • I can add and subtract geometric vectors with the triangle and parallelogram laws and find resultants with the cosine law.
  • I can describe the effect of scalar multiplication, test for collinearity, and build unit vectors.
  • I can write vectors in component form in R² and R³, convert to and from i, j, k form, and compute magnitudes.
  • I can add, subtract, and scale algebraic vectors componentwise, and find AB from two points.
  • I can write linear combinations, test spanning sets, and decide whether a vector lies in a spanned plane.
