UNIT 4 OVERVIEW

Curve Sketching

Textbook: Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 4 (PDF pp. ~161–220). Strand B — Characteristics of Functions via Derivatives. Suggested pacing: 12–14 periods.

Units 1–3 gave you limits, derivative rules, and optimization. This unit turns the derivative into a graphing instrument: the sign of f′ maps every rise and fall (and classifies hills and valleys), the sign of f″ maps every bend (and spots where the bending reverses), and limits map the invisible guard rails — vertical, horizontal, and oblique asymptotes. The six-step algorithm assembles all of it into accurate hand-drawn sketches of polynomial and rational functions, and the capstone reads those same features off real-world graphs, where a local maximum is a sell signal and an inflection point marks the moment momentum shifts.

Ontario curriculum expectations (Chapter 4, Strand B)

  • Determine intervals of increase/decrease and local extrema of polynomial and rational functions using the first derivative (4.1).
  • Determine concavity and points of inflection using the second derivative; apply the second derivative test (4.2, 4.3).
  • Determine vertical, horizontal, and oblique asymptotes using limits, including limits at infinity (4.4).
  • Sketch the graphs of polynomial and rational functions by applying an algorithm that uses intercepts, symmetry, asymptotes, critical points, and concavity (4.5).
  • Interpret the key features of applied graphs (maxima, minima, inflection points) and connect curve sketching to optimization (4.6).

🎯 Learning Goals

  • I can find where a function increases or decreases from the sign of f′, and classify critical points with the first derivative test.
  • I can find where a function is concave up or down from the sign of f″, classify extrema with the second derivative test, and locate points of inflection.
  • I can find vertical, horizontal, and oblique asymptotes using limits.
  • I can run the six-step curve sketching algorithm to produce accurate, fully labelled graphs of polynomial and rational functions.
  • I can read maxima, minima, and inflection points off applied graphs and explain what they mean in context.
