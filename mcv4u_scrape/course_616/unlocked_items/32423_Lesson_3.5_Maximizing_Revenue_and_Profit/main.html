Maximizing Revenue and Profit

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §3.4 (part 1) — revenue = price × quantity, and calculus finds the sweet spot.

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Build a revenue function R = (price)(quantity) from market research.
  • Maximize revenue on a practical domain (capacity limits, integer price steps).
  • Use marginal revenue R′(x) and marginal cost C′(x) to analyze profit.

💡 Minds On

In business, it is extremely important to manage costs effectively: good control allows minimization of costs and maximization of profit. At the same time there are human considerations — maximizing profit while antagonizing customers can hurt future profits. So in addition to mathematical constraints, consider practical constraints on the domain when you construct a workable function. Recall: revenue = (price per unit) × (number of units), and profit, cost, and revenue are analyzed per unit produced or sold, with marginal cost = C′(x) and marginal revenue = R′(x).

📖 Learn — step by step

Example 1 — A commuter train carries 2000 passengers daily from a suburb into a large city. The fare is $7.00 per person. Market research shows that 40 fewer people would ride the train for each $0.10 increase in the fare, and 40 more people would ride for each $0.10 decrease. The train's capacity is 2600 passengers, and carrying fewer than 1600 passengers means costs exceed revenue. What fare should the railway charge to get the largest possible revenue?

Solution. To maximize revenue, we need a revenue function: revenue = (number of passengers) × (fare per passenger).
Let x be the number of $0.10 fare increases (x = 3 means a $0.30 increase; x = −1 means a $0.10 decrease). Then:
• the fare per passenger is 7 + 0.10x,
• the number of passengers is 2000 − 40x.
Since passengers must be at least 1600: 2000 − 40x ≥ 1600, so x ≤ 10. Since capacity is 2600: 2000 − 40x ≤ 2600, so x ≥ −15. The domain is −15 ≤ x ≤ 10.
The revenue function is R(x) = (7 + 0.10x)(2000 − 40x) = −4x² − 80x + 14 000.
Practically, x must be an integer (fares move in $0.10 steps). To find the absolute maximum on −15 ≤ x ≤ 10:
R′(x) = −8x − 80; R′(x) = 0 when x = −10. R′(x) is never undefined, and x = −10 is in the domain. Evaluate:
R(−15) = −4(−15)² − 80(−15) + 14 000 = 14 300;
R(−10) = −4(−10)² − 80(−10) + 14 000 = 14 400;
R(10) = −4(10)² − 80(10) + 14 000 = 12 800.
The maximum revenue occurs at x = −10: ten $0.10 fare decreases, i.e., a fare decrease of $1.00. At a fare of $6.00, daily revenue is $14 400 with 2400 passengers.

Profit analysis. With profit P(x) = R(x) − C(x), the instantaneous rate of change of profit is zero when marginal revenue equals marginal cost: P′(x) = R′(x) − C′(x) = 0. That is where profit stops growing — the peak of the profit curve.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 3.4.

Question 1 — CD players sell at $90 (50 units per 2 weeks); each $1 price cut sells 5 more units. What price maximizes revenue?

Let x = number of $1 decreases: R(x) = (90 − x)(50 + 5x) = −5x² + 400x + 4500. R′ = −10x + 400 = 0 → x = 40. Price: $50.

Question 2 — A 20 000-seat arena averages 14 000 fans at $75/ticket; each $5 ticket cut adds 800 fans. What ticket price maximizes revenue?

Let x = number of $5 decreases: R(x) = (75 − 5x)(14 000 + 800x) = −4000x² − 10 000x + 1 050 000. R′ = −8000x − 10 000 = 0 → x = −1.25. Negative x means a price increase: price = 75 + 6.25 = $81.25.

Question 3 — MP3 players: $100 price → 120/month; +$2 price → 1 fewer sold; store cost $70 each. Find the profit-maximizing price.

Let x = number of $2 increases: units = 120 − x, profit per unit = 30 + 2x. P(x) = (120 − x)(30 + 2x) = −2x² + 210x + 3600. P′ = −4x + 210 = 0 → x = 52.5. Price = 100 + 2(52.5) = $205.

Question 4 — With P(x) = R(x) − C(x), show that P′(x) = 0 when marginal revenue equals marginal cost.

P′(x) = R′(x) − C′(x) = 0 → R′(x) = C′(x).

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • Revenue = (price)(quantity) — build R(x) from the market research, then maximize it.
  • Respect practical domain constraints: capacity limits, minimum viable sales, integer price steps.

Need to know

  • Marginal revenue = R′(x), marginal cost = C′(x); profit peaks where they are equal.
