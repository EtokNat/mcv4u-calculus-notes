UNIT 3 OVERVIEW

Unit 3 — Derivatives and Their Applications

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 3 (pp. 115–160) — putting derivatives to work.

Textbook: Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 3 (pp. 115–160). Strand B — Derivatives and Their Applications. Suggested pacing: 12–14 periods.

Unit 2 gave you the derivative as a tool. This unit puts it to work: the second derivative describes how a rate itself is changing, velocity and acceleration turn derivatives into motion, the extreme-value algorithm finds the best possible outcome on an interval, and optimization solves real problems — from the biggest field a fence can enclose to the fare that maximizes a transit company's revenue.

Ontario curriculum expectations (Chapter 3)

  • Make connections between the concept of motion and the concept of derivatives (3.1).
  • Solve problems involving rates of change (3.1).
  • Determine second derivatives (3.1).
  • Determine the extreme values of a function on an interval (3.2).
  • Apply mathematical models and their derivatives to determine, interpret, and communicate results when solving problems (3.3).
  • Determine the maximum and minimum values of a mathematical model, including in economics and science contexts (3.3, 3.4).

Learning goals

  • I can compute second derivatives and use correct notation (f″(x), d²y/dx²).
  • I can describe straight-line motion with position, velocity, and acceleration functions, and tell when an object is at rest, speeding up, or slowing down.
  • I can find absolute maximum and minimum values of a function on a closed interval using the extreme-value algorithm.
  • I can set up and solve optimization problems (area, volume, distance) with a labelled diagram and a justified domain.
  • I can use marginal cost, marginal revenue, and marginal profit to maximize revenue/profit and minimize cost, and interpret each result in context.

How you will be assessed

  • Entry Card / Exit Card — short surveys (for learning).
  • Formative Checks A, B, C — practice quizzes with feedback on every answer (for learning, 0 points).
  • Unit 3 Summative Assignment — 16 questions, 100 points (of learning).
  • Unit 3 Summative Test — 26 questions, 100 points, 120-minute limit (of learning).
