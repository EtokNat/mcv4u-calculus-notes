UNIT 5 OVERVIEW

Derivatives of Exponential and Trigonometric Functions

Textbook: Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12, Chapter 5 (PDF pp. 221–272, verified). Strand B — Derivatives and Their Applications. Suggested pacing: 12–14 periods.

Units 1–4 gave you limits, the derivative rules, and curve sketching. This unit extends differentiation to two new function families. First, exponentials: there is a special number e ≈ 2.718 whose exponential function is its own derivative — the simplest rule in calculus — and every other base b^x differentiates to a stretched copy of itself, b^x·ln b. Then, trigonometry: the slope of a sine wave traces cosine, the slope of cosine traces negative sine, and the derivative of tangent is sec²x. The unit closes with a capstone where both families model the real world: exponential models describe population growth, radioactive decay, and optimal advertising campaigns; sinusoidal models describe springs and harmonic motion, where the second derivative turns out to be a negative multiple of the position — the signature equation of oscillation.

Ontario curriculum expectations (Chapter 5, Strand B)

  • Define the number e and determine the derivative of y = e^x (5.1).
  • Determine the derivative of the general exponential function y = b^x (5.2).
  • Compare the graph of an exponential function with the graph of its derivative (5.1, 5.2).
  • Solve optimization problems using exponential functions (5.3).
  • Investigate and determine the derivatives of sinusoidal functions (5.4).
  • Determine the derivative of the tangent function (5.5).
  • Solve rate-of-change problems involving exponential and trigonometric models using their derivatives (5.1–5.5).

🎯 Learning Goals

  • I can differentiate e^x and e^(g(x)) with the chain rule, and explain why the power rule does not apply to variable exponents.
  • I can differentiate b^x and b^(g(x)) using d/dx(b^x) = b^x·ln b, and interpret growth vs decay from the sign of ln b.
  • I can solve optimization problems with exponential functions using the extreme-value algorithm.
  • I can differentiate sin x, cos x, and tan x (with the chain, product, and quotient rules) and find tangents and extrema of sinusoidal functions.
  • I can model growth/decay with P(t) = P₀e^(rt) (doubling time, half-life) and harmonic motion with s(t) = A sin kt, verifying s″ + k²s = 0.
