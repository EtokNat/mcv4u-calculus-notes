🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Compute work as W = F·d and interpret its sign.
  • Compute torque as τ = r × F — magnitude |r||F|sinθ and direction from the right-hand rule.
  • Solve area problems with the cross product.
  • Choose the right product for a situation: dot for a scalar answer, cross for a vector (or area) answer.

💡 Minds On

Push a lawnmower handle straight down and nothing moves forward — all effort, no work. Push it at a shallow angle and it glides. Work is not just "force" — it is force in the direction of motion. That is a dot product. But loosening a bolt needs a twist, not a push — that is a cross product. Today you put both products to work.

🔍 Explore — only the along-part works

  1. A force of 10 N pushes a crate along a horizontal displacement of 8 m, but the push is angled 30° above the horizontal. Define F=(10cos(30°),10sin(30°)) and d=(8,0). Compute the work W=F[1]d[1]+F[2]d[2] — you should get ≈ 69.28 J.
  2. Now change the angle: edit F to use 80° instead of 30°. The work drops to ≈ 13.9 J. At 90° it becomes 0 — all push, no progress.
  3. Conclusion: work = F·d = |F||d|cosθ; only the force component along the displacement does work. The cross product, by contrast, measures the twisting the force would cause.

📖 Learn — step by step

Work: the dot product in physics

When a constant force F moves an object through a displacement d, the work done is

W = F · d = |F| |d| cosθ  (joules)

Only the component of F along d counts — which is exactly what the dot product computes. The sign tells a story: positive work means the force helps the motion; negative work means the force opposes it (friction does negative work); zero work means the force is perpendicular to the motion.

Torque: the cross product in physics

When a force F is applied at a position r from a pivot, the torque (the turning effect) is

τ = r × F,    |τ| = |r| |F| sinθ  (newton-metres)

The magnitude is biggest when the force is perpendicular to the lever arm (θ = 90°) and zero when the force points along the arm. The direction — given by the right-hand rule — tells you the axis of rotation and the sense of turning (for example, "out of the page" = counterclockwise when viewed from that side).

Which product? A decision guide

  • Want a number? Use the dot product: work, angles, projections, testing perpendicularity.
  • Want a perpendicular vector or an area? Use the cross product: torque, areas of parallelograms and triangles, a vector normal to two others.
  • Zero test: a·b = 0 (nonzero) means perpendicular; a × b = 0 (nonzero) means parallel.
  • Order: the dot product commutes; the cross product flips sign when you swap the order.

Example 1 — A force F = (4, −2, 3) N displaces an object by d = (2, 5, −1) m. Find the work done and interpret the sign.

W = F·d = (4)(2) + (−2)(5) + (3)(−1) = 8 − 10 − 3 = −5 J. The negative work means the force opposes the displacement overall — it is taking energy out of the motion rather than adding it.

Example 2 — A wrench applies a force F = (0, 20, 0) N at a point r = (0.5, 0, 0) m from the bolt. Find the torque vector and its magnitude.

τ = r × F = ((0)(0) − (0)(20), (0)(0) − (0.5)(0), (0.5)(20) − (0)(0)) = (0, 0, 10) N·m. The torque points along +z (out of the page in the diagram), and its magnitude 10 N·m matches |r||F|sin90° = (0.5)(20)(1) = 10.

Example 3 — Find the area of the triangle with vertices A(2, 1, 0), B(5, 3, 1), and C(1, 4, 2).

Build edge vectors from A: AB = (3, 2, 1) and AC = (−1, 3, 2). AB × AC = ((2)(2) − (1)(3), (1)(−1) − (3)(2), (3)(3) − (2)(−1)) = (4 − 3, −1 − 6, 9 + 2) = (1, −7, 11). Magnitude = √(1 + 49 + 121) = √171 ≈ 13.077. Triangle area = √171/2 ≈ 6.54 square units.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on §7.7 application problems.

Question 1 — A force F = (6, 2, −1) N moves an object through d = (3, −4, 2) m. Find the work.

W = (6)(3) + (2)(−4) + (−1)(2) = 18 − 8 − 2 = 8 J.

Question 2 — Find the torque τ = r × F for r = (2, 0, 0) m and F = (0, −5, 0) N. Which way does it point?

τ = ((0)(0) − (0)(−5), (0)(2) − (2)(0), (2)(−5) − (0)(0)) = (0, 0, −10) N·m — along −z (into the page), magnitude 10 N·m.

Question 3 — You need a vector perpendicular to both u = (1, 2, 0) and v = (0, 1, 3). Dot product or cross product — and why?

Cross product. The dot product returns a scalar; the cross product returns a vector perpendicular to both — exactly what is asked for. u × v = ((2)(3) − (0)(1), (0)(0) − (1)(3), (1)(1) − (2)(0)) = (6, −3, 1).

Question 4 — A 50 N force pushes a box 12 m horizontally, angled 40° above the horizontal. How much work is done?

W = |F||d|cosθ = (50)(12)cos40° ≈ 600(0.766) ≈ 459.6 J.

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • Work W = F·d — a scalar; positive helps the motion, negative opposes it, zero is perpendicular.
  • Torque τ = r × F — a vector; magnitude |r||F|sinθ, direction from the right-hand rule.
  • Area comes from |a × b| (parallelogram) or half of it (triangle).

Need to know

  • Dot product → number (work, angle, projection, perpendicularity test). Cross product → vector or area (torque, parallel test).
  • The cross product's sign flip (a × b = −b × a) means direction answers must respect order.
