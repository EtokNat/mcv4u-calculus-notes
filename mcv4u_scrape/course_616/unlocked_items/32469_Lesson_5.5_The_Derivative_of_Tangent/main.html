The Derivative of Tangent

Nelson Calculus and Vectors 12 §5.5 — deriving d/dx(tan x) = sec²x from sine and cosine, then chaining it through composite functions.

🎯 Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Derive d/dx(tan x) = sec²x from d/dx(sin x) and d/dx(cos x).
  • Apply d/dx(tan f(x)) = sec²f(x)·f′(x), alone or combined with product/quotient rules.
  • Explain why tan x has no horizontal tangents (sec²x ≥ 1 always).
  • Find tangent lines to tangent curves and locate their extrema.

💡 Minds On

We have derivatives for sin x and cos x. Since tan x = sin x / cos x, its derivative must already be hiding inside the rules we know — no new investigation needed. The result, sec²x, has a lovely consequence: because sec²x = 1/cos²x ≥ 1, a tangent curve is always climbing (except at its asymptotes). It never has a horizontal tangent — a fact you can use to check your work instantly.

📖 Learn — step by step

Deriving the rule from sine and cosine

Write tan x as sin x·(cos x)−1 and apply the product rule:

Example 1 — Prove that d/dx(tan x) = sec²x.

Solution. With tan x = sin x·(cos x)−1, the product rule gives
d/dx(tan x) = (cos x)(cos x)−1 + (sin x)(−1)(cos x)−2(−sin x)
= 1 + sin²x/cos²x = 1 + tan²x.

By the Pythagorean identity, 1 + tan²x = sec²x. ∎

RULE — Derivative of tan x
d/dx(tan x) = sec²x (equivalently 1 + tan²x).
Chain-rule form: d/dx(tan f(x)) = sec²f(x)·f′(x).

Using the rule with the chain, product, and quotient rules

Example 2 — Differentiate y = tan(x² + 3x).

Solution. Chain rule: y′ = sec²(x² + 3x)·(2x + 3) = (2x + 3)sec²(x² + 3x).

Example 3 — Determine the equation of the tangent to y = tan x at x = π/4.

Solution. Point: (π/4, 1). Slope: y′ = sec²x, so y′(π/4) = sec²(π/4) = (√2)² = 2.
Tangent: y − 1 = 2(x − π/4), i.e. y = 2x + 1 − π/2.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 5.5.

Question 1 — Differentiate y = tan 4x.

Chain rule: y′ = 4 sec²4x.

Question 2 — Differentiate y = tan²x.

Think of (tan x)²: y′ = 2 tan x·sec²x.

Question 3 — True or false: "tan x has horizontal tangents." Justify.

False. d/dx(tan x) = sec²x = 1/cos²x ≥ 1 for every x in the domain — the slope is never zero, so tan x is always increasing and never has a horizontal tangent.

Question 4 — Find the tangent to y = 2 tan x at x = π/4.

Point: (π/4, 2). Slope: y′ = 2 sec²x, so y′(π/4) = 2·2 = 4. Tangent: y − 2 = 4(x − π/4).

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • d/dx(tan x) = sec²x (= 1 + tan²x) — derived from sine and cosine via the product rule.
  • Chain-rule form: d/dx(tan f(x)) = sec²f(x)·f′(x).
  • Since sec²x ≥ 1, tan x is always increasing — no horizontal tangents, ever.
  • Combine freely with product/quotient/chain rules, e.g. (sin x + tan x)4 and x·tan(2x − 1).

Need to know

  • At the asymptotes (x = π/2, 3π/2, …) tan x is undefined — restrict the domain before analyzing.
