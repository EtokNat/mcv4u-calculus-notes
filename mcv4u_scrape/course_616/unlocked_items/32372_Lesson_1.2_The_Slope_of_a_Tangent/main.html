The Slope of a Tangent

🎯 Learning Goals

💡 Minds On

You already know what a tangent to a curve is — but what geometric meaning can we give it at a point P? A tangent is the straight line that most resembles the graph near a point, and its slope tells you how steep the graph is at the point of tangency. Below, four tangents touch the same curve:

The goal of this section is to build a method for finding that slope at any given point. We start with a quick review of lines and slopes.

📖 Learn — step by step

Lines and slopes — a review

The slope m of the line joining P₁(x₁, y₁) and P₂(x₂, y₂) is m = Δy/Δx = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁). A line's equation can be written in point–slope form as (y − y₁)/(x − x₁) = m, or y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), and in slope–y-intercept form as y = mx + b.

To write the equation of a tangent to a curve at a point, we first need the tangent's slope. But the slope formula needs two points, and we only have one — point P. What can we do?

The secant idea

Take a second point Q on the curve. The line joining P and Q is called a secant. Now imagine Q as a moving point that slides along the curve toward P — the secant's slope becomes a better and better estimate of the tangent's slope:

Slope of a Tangent — definition. The slope of the tangent to a curve at a point P is the limiting slope of the secant PQ as the point Q slides along the curve toward P. In other words, the tangent slope is the limit of the secant slope as Q approaches P along the curve.

Since we care about points Q very close to P(3, 9) on y = x², it is convenient to write Q as (3 + h, (3 + h)²), where h is a very small nonzero number. As Q slides toward P, h takes values closer and closer to zero — we say "h approaches zero" and write h → 0. The notation limh→0 means "limiting value as h approaches 0". So the tangent slope at P is limh→0 (slope of the secant PQ).

Example 1 — Determine the slope of the tangent to the parabola f(x) = x² at P(3, 9).

Solution. Using P(3, 9) and Q(3 + h, (3 + h)²), h ≠ 0, the slope of the secant PQ is
(y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)   (substitute)
= [(3 + h)² − 9]/[(3 + h) − 3]   (expand)
= [9 + 6h + h² − 9]/h   (simplify and factor)
= h(6 + h)/h
= (6 + h)   (divide by the common factor of h)

As h → 0, the value of (6 + h) approaches 6, and thus limh→0(6 + h) = 6.
We conclude that the slope of the tangent at P(3, 9) to the parabola y = x² is 6.

Example 2 — Estimating with a series of secants. Consider y = −(1/8)(x + 1)(x − 7).

Solution.
a. The x-intercepts are −1 and 7, so the axis of symmetry is x = (−1 + 7)/2 = 3. Substituting x = 3 gives y = −(1/8)(4)(−4) = 2, so the vertex is (3, 2). The y-intercept is 7/8. Integer-x points on the parabola: (−1, 0), (0, 0.875), (1, 1.5), (2, 1.875), (3, 2), (4, 1.875), (5, 1.5), (6, 0.875).
b. The secant through (−1, 0) and P(5, 1.5) has slope m = (1.5 − 0)/(5 − (−1)) = 0.25. Using the other points with P(5, 1.5), the slopes are 0.125, 0, −0.125, −0.25, −0.375, and −0.625, respectively.
c. The tangent slope at P(5, 1.5) lies between −0.375 and −0.625; using points closer and closer to P(5, 1.5) it can be determined to be −0.5.

The slope of a tangent at an arbitrary point — the difference quotient

Now we generalize. Let P(a, f(a)) be a fixed point on y = f(x), and let Q be a horizontal distance h away: Q(a + h, f(a + h)). The secant slope is Δy/Δx = [f(a + h) − f(a)]/[(a + h) − a] = [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h. This quotient is fundamental to calculus and is called the difference quotient. The slope m of the tangent at P(a, f(a)) is limh→0 (slope of the secant PQ), i.e. m = limh→0 [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h.

Slope of a Tangent as a Limit. The slope of the tangent to y = f(x) at P(a, f(a)) is m = limΔx→0 Δy/Δx = limh→0 [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h, if this limit exists.

Example 3 — a. Using the definition, determine the tangent slope to y = −x² + 4x + 1 at the point where x = 3. b. Determine the tangent's equation. c. Sketch the graph and the tangent.

Solution.
a. Here f(x) = −x² + 4x + 1 and a = 3. Then f(3) = −(3)² + 4(3) + 1 = 4, and
f(3 + h) = −(3 + h)² + 4(3 + h) + 1 = −9 − 6h − h² + 12 + 4h + 1 = −h² − 2h + 4.
The tangent slope at (3, 4) is
m = limh→0 [f(3 + h) − f(3)]/h   (substitute)
= limh→0 [(−h² − 2h + 4) − 4]/h   (simplify and factor)
= limh→0 h(−h − 2)/h   (divide by the common factor)
= limh→0 (−h − 2)   (evaluate)
= −2.
b. The tangent at (3, 4) satisfies (y − 4)/(x − 3) = −2, or y = −2x + 10.
c. (Sketch the parabola y = −x² + 4x + 1 and the line y = −2x + 10 through (3, 4).)

Example 4 — Determine the tangent slope to the rational function f(x) = (3x + 6)/x at the point (2, 6).

Solution.
m = limh→0 [f(2 + h) − f(2)]/h   (substitute)
= limh→0 [(6 + 3h + 6)/(2 + h) − 6]/h   (determine a common denominator)
= limh→0 [(6 + 3h + 6)/(2 + h) − 6(2 + h)/(2 + h)]/h   (simplify)
= limh→0 [(12 + 3h − 12 − 6h)/(2 + h)]/h
= limh→0 [(−3h)/(2 + h)]/h   (multiply by the reciprocal)
= limh→0 (−3h)/(2 + h) × 1/h
= limh→0 −3/(2 + h)   (evaluate)
= −1.5.
Therefore, the tangent slope to f(x) = (3x + 6)/x at (2, 6) is −1.5.

Example 5 — Find the tangent slope to f(x) = √x at x = 9. (Watch for your Lesson 1.1 skill!)

Solution. f(9) = √9 = 3 and f(9 + h) = √(9 + h). Then
m = limh→0 [f(9 + h) − f(9)]/h   (substitute)
= limh→0 [√(9 + h) − 3]/h   (rationalize the numerator)
= limh→0 [√(9 + h) − 3]/h × [√(9 + h) + 3]/[√(9 + h) + 3]
= limh→0 [(9 + h) − 9]/[h(√(9 + h) + 3)]   (simplify)
= limh→0 h/[h(√(9 + h) + 3)]   (divide by the common factor of h)
= limh→0 1/[√(9 + h) + 3]   (evaluate)
= 1/6.
Therefore, the tangent slope to f(x) = √x at x = 9 is 1/6.

✅ Check Your Understanding

Try these on paper first, then reveal. Modelled on Exercise 1.2.

Question 1 — Calculate the slope of the line through (2, 7) and (−3, −8).

m = (−8 − 7)/(−3 − 2) = (−15)/(−5) = 3.

Question 2 — Using the definition, find the tangent slope to y = x² at x = 2.

m = limh→0 [(2 + h)² − 4]/h = limh→0 (4h + h²)/h = limh→0 (4 + h) = 4.

Question 3 — Find the equation of the tangent to y = x² − 3x + 1 at (3, 1).

m = limh→0 [((3+h)² − 3(3+h) + 1) − 1]/h = limh→0 (3h + h²)/h = 3. Tangent: y − 1 = 3(x − 3), i.e. y = 3x − 8.

Question 4 — Explain in your own words how to approximate a tangent slope without using the limit definition.

Pick several points Q on the curve near P, compute each secant slope, and watch the values settle toward a number as Q gets closer to P — that number estimates the tangent slope (as in Example 2).

📝 Lesson Summary

Key ideas

  • The tangent slope at P is the limit of secant slopes as Q moves closer to P: mtangent = limQ→P(slope of secant PQ).
  • At P(a, f(a)): mtangent = limΔx→0 Δy/Δx = limh→0 [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h.

Need to know — the procedure

  • To find the tangent slope at P(a, f(a)): find f(a); find f(a + h); evaluate limh→0 [f(a + h) − f(a)]/h.
  • Simplify the difference quotient first (expand, factor, rationalize) — never substitute h = 0 until the h in the denominator is gone.
