Learning Goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

  • Compute second derivatives by differentiating twice.
  • Use the notations \(f''(x)\), \(y''\), and \(\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}\) correctly.
  • Explain why the second derivative is written as \(\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right)\).

Minds On

Derivatives arise in the study of motion. The velocity of a car is the rate of change of displacement at a specific point in time.

We have developed the rules of differentiation — now we extend them to higher-order derivatives.

Differentiating the first derivative gives the second derivative, which will describe acceleration and much more.

Part 1 — What a second derivative is

Higher-order derivatives

If \(y = f(x)\) has first derivative \(f'(x)\), its second derivative is the derivative of \(f'(x)\):

\[\dfrac{d}{dx}\!\left(\dfrac{dy}{dx}\right) = \dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}.\]

All these notations mean the same thing:

\(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = f'(x) = y'\)   and   \(\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = f''(x) = y''.\)

Two quick warm-ups

(a) If \(f(x) = 10x^{4}\), then \(f'(x) = 40x^{3}\) and \(f''(x) = \boxed{120x^{2}}.\)

(b) If \(y = 2x^{3} - 5x^{2}\), then \(\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 6x^{2} - 10x\) and \(\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} = \boxed{12x - 10}.\)

Rule: differentiate once to get \(f'\), differentiate the result to get \(f''\).

Part 2 — Worked example

Example 1 — \(f(x) = \dfrac{x}{1+x}\). Find \(f''(1)\).

Step 1. Rewrite as a product: \(f(x) = x(x+1)^{-1}.\)

Step 2. Product + power-of-a-function rule:

\(f'(x) = (1)(x+1)^{-1} + (x)(-1)(x+1)^{-2}(1)\)

\(= \dfrac{1}{x+1} - \dfrac{x}{(x+1)^{2}}.\)

Example 1 — simplify \(f'(x)\) before differentiating again

\(f'(x) = \dfrac{(x+1) - x}{(x+1)^{2}} = \dfrac{1}{(1+x)^{2}}.\)

Rewrite as a power: \(f'(x) = (1+x)^{-2}.\)

Why simplify first? A clean \(f'\) saves a mountain of algebra when you differentiate again.

Example 1 — differentiate again

\(f'(x) = (1+x)^{-2}.\)

Power of a function: \(f''(x) = -2(1+x)^{-3}(1)\)

\(= \dfrac{-2}{(1+x)^{3}}.\)

At \(x = 1\): \(f''(1) = \dfrac{-2}{(1+1)^{3}} = \dfrac{-2}{8}.\)

\(f''(1) = -\dfrac{1}{4}.\)

Classwork

Four questions — try each on paper first, then reveal the answer.

Modelled on Exercise 3.1 from Bronston's lesson page.

Allocate ~10 min total. If student is fast, they finish within the lesson.

C1 — Find \(f''(x)\) for \(f(x) = x^{10} + 3x^{6}\)

Step 1. \(f'(x) = 10x^{9} + 18x^{5}.\)

Step 2. Differentiate again.

\(f''(x) = 90x^{8} + 90x^{4}.\)

C2 — Find \(f''(x)\) for \(f(x) = \sqrt{x}\)

Step 1. Rewrite: \(f(x) = x^{1/2}.\)

Step 2. \(f'(x) = \tfrac{1}{2}x^{-1/2}.\)

Step 3. Differentiate again.

\(f''(x) = -\dfrac{1}{4}x^{-3/2}.\)

C3 — Find \(f''(x)\) for \(f(x) = (1-x)^{2}\)

Step 1. Expand: \(f(x) = 1 - 2x + x^{2}.\)

Step 2. \(f'(x) = -2 + 2x.\)

Step 3. Differentiate again.

\(f''(x) = 2.\)

A constant — the curve has uniform concavity.

C4 — Find \(f''(1)\) for \(f(x) = \dfrac{2x}{x+5}\)

Step 1. Quotient rule, then simplify: \(f'(x) = \dfrac{10}{(x+5)^{2}} = 10(x+5)^{-2}.\)

Step 2. \(f''(x) = -20(x+5)^{-3}.\)

Step 3. At \(x = 1\): \(f''(1) = -\dfrac{20}{6^{3}} = -\dfrac{20}{216}.\)

\(f''(1) = -\dfrac{5}{54}.\)

Wrap-up — Lesson 3.1

Key ideas

  • The second derivative is the derivative of the first derivative.
  • Notations: \(f''(x)\), \(y''\), \(\dfrac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}}\) — the superscript records "derivative of the derivative."

Need to know

  • Simplify \(f'(x)\) (e.g. rewrite as a power function) before differentiating again.
